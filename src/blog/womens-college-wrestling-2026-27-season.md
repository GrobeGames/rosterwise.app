> **Status as of October 8, 2026.** Everything below comes from NCAA documents and from the schools' own announcements, read on October 8, 2026. Where the NCAA has not yet said something, we say so rather than guess. We will update this post as the NCAA publishes more.

The 2026-27 season is the second in which the NCAA crowns women's wrestling champions. It is also the **last season in which Division I, II, and III programs share one championship**. If your athlete is a high school junior or senior, or already wrestles in college, a few things changed this year that are worth knowing.

## The 2027 championship: dates and site

The second **National Collegiate Women's Wrestling Championships** are set for **March 5–6, 2027, at Xtream Arena in Coralville, Iowa**, the same arena that hosted the first championship in March 2026.

"National Collegiate" means wrestlers from Division I, II, and III programs qualify into a single field rather than separate division championships. The ten weight classes are unchanged: 103, 110, 117, 124, 131, 138, 145, 160, 180, and 207 pounds.

## Regionals: six sites, 180 qualifiers

Wrestlers reach the championship through **six regional tournaments on February 19–21, 2027**. The NCAA's 2026-27 bid specifications allocate **180 qualifying positions, 30 from each regional**.

The NCAA Women's Wrestling Committee approved these hosts on June 22, 2026:

| Region | Host | Facility | Dates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One | Ithaca | Ben Light Gymnasium | Feb. 19–20 |
| Two | Lehigh | Rauch Fieldhouse | Feb. 19–20 |
| Three | Emmanuel (Ga.) | Shaw Athletic Center | Feb. 19–20 |
| Four | Tiffin | Heminger Center | Feb. 19–20 |
| Five | Wisconsin-Stevens Point | Quandt Fieldhouse | Feb. 20–21 |
| Six | Fort Hays State | Gross Memorial Coliseum | Feb. 19–20 |

One cost detail for families to know: the committee's July 2026 report says that for women's wrestling regionals, **institutions are responsible for all of their own travel**, including transportation, food, and lodging. For some teams that means a flight.

## Which schools are in which region

On **July 23, 2026**, the committee assigned institutions to the six regions for 2027. Its stated goal was to "balance the number of teams per region as closely as possible while adhering to geographical proximity." The committee said it will review the assignments every year as sponsorship grows, and that it will keep looking for ways to increase the number of qualifiers. For 2027, the bid specifications set the number of qualifiers at 180.

The alignment attached to that July vote lists **120 institutions**, 20 per region. The 2026-27 alignment now posted on the NCAA's championship page lists **128** when we checked on October 8, 2026. Either way, **more than 100 institutions** are placed in the 2027 regionals. The alignment covers only schools competing this season, so it does not include the Division I programs below that start in 2027-28.

Sponsorship counts vary by document and date, so we don't print one national total. The most recent NCAA sponsorship figure we found is from January 2026, when the NCAA said **55 Division III schools** sponsored women's wrestling, "more than half of the schools sponsoring the sport."

## 2027 is the last combined year for Division III

The NCAA's 2026-27 bid specifications say it plainly: **"2027 will be the last year with Division III institutions competing in the National Collegiate Championships."**

In January 2026, Division III delegates at the NCAA Convention established the **Division III Women's Wrestling Championships, starting in the 2027-28 academic year**. The March 2026 report of the women's wrestling committee says a separate Division III committee was being formed and would "work out the details" of that championship.

What we could not find: the NCAA has not published, in anything we read, the date, site, field size, or qualifying format of the first Division III championship. The bid specifications also do not describe how the National Collegiate championship will be structured for Divisions I and II once Division III leaves. We won't guess. Ask Division III coaches what they have been told (see the questions below).

Our reading of the dates, not an NCAA statement: a wrestler who enrolls at a Division III school in fall 2027 would compete under the Division III championship from her first college season.

## New Division I programs

Several Division I schools have announced women's wrestling programs since December 2025. Here are four, with each detail taken from the school's own announcement.

- **Mercyhurst University (Erie, Pa.)** announced on January 28, 2026 that women's wrestling would begin in the **2026-27 academic year** as a Division I program. Mercyhurst appears in Region Two of the 2027 alignment.
- **Kent State University** announced on December 18, 2025 that its program **will begin competition in fall 2027**. Kent State describes itself as the first Division I institution in Ohio to sponsor the sport.
- **Iowa State University** announced on April 16, 2026 that women's wrestling will debut in the **2027-28 academic year**, with **10 scholarships and a roster of 30 positions** on its first team. Alli St. John is the program's first head coach.
- **Columbia University** announced on June 10, 2026 that women's wrestling becomes a varsity sport **beginning with the 2027-28 academic year**, elevating its existing club team. Columbia describes itself as the first Ivy League institution to offer the sport at the varsity level, and plans a roster of approximately 30 for its first team. Emma Randall is head coach.

For context on roster size: at Division I schools covered by the House settlement, NCAA Bylaw 17.2 limits women's wrestling rosters to **30**. That is a ceiling on roster size, not a promise of scholarships. How many scholarships a program funds is its own decision, which is why Iowa State's announcement of 10 scholarships for a 30-person roster is a useful example of the difference. See our [scholarships guide](/wrestling/guide/scholarships-and-the-house-settlement/) for more.

## An open question: who writes the women's rules

Women's college wrestling is freestyle, and today its rules book is not written by an NCAA rules committee the way the men's book is.

At its March 23–24, 2026 annual meeting, the NCAA Women's Wrestling Committee recommended adding an **NCAA women's wrestling playing rules book, a secretary-rules editor, and a rules committee, effective January 1, 2027**. Its stated reason was the Division III championship arriving in 2028. The report says an outside group of women's wrestling stakeholders currently updates the book, and that the group has said it does not want to keep doing so for much longer.

The NCAA's playing-rules schedule, updated September 17, 2026, still lists women's wrestling under "Non-NCAA Rules Sources," with the National Wrestling Coaches Association Leadership Group and Executive Committee as the rules source and an annual meeting in March 2027.

We did not find an NCAA record that the January 1, 2027 change has been approved. Until one appears, treat it as undecided. For families, the practical point is that the rules your athlete competes under in 2026-27 are the ones in the 2026–27 National Collegiate Women's Wrestling Rules Book.

## What has not changed

- **Weight classes.** The same ten weights as 2026.
- **Weight management.** The NCAA's 2026–27 women's weight-management packet, dated August 18, 2026, says "there are no changes to the rules related to weight loss descent." The standards remain a urine specific gravity of 1.020 or lower before assessment, a minimum wrestling weight set at **17% body fat**, and a descent limit of **1.5% of body weight per week**. Our [weight-management guide](/wrestling/guide/weight-management-and-safety/) explains each one.
- **Recruiting dates.** In April 2026 the NCAA adopted later Division I recruiting dates (Proposal 2026-12), but the legislation is written for **men's** wrestling only. It does not change women's wrestling.
- **Transfer window.** The Division I transfer window change announced January 14, 2026 (30 days beginning April 1) is for **men's** wrestling. The NCAA's announcement does not mention women's wrestling.

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Which regional will your team wrestle at in February 2027, and how does the team travel there?"** Schools pay their own regional travel, so the answer tells you something about the program's budget and schedule.
2. **For Division III programs: "What have you been told about the Division III championship starting in 2027-28 — format, qualifying, and timing?"** The NCAA has not published those details yet, so listen for whether the staff is candid about what is unknown.
3. **For new programs: "When does your first class enroll, how many roster spots are you filling, and how many scholarships are funded?"** A new program's roster is open by definition, but the funding and timeline vary by school.
4. **For Division I programs: "How many of your roster spots do you fund, and at what level?"** The roster limit is 30 at schools in the settlement; funding is a separate question.
5. **"How does your staff handle weight certification and descent?"** How a staff talks about weight tells you something about the room your athlete would join.

## What this means for your family

For 2026-27, the championship runs the way it did last year: one field, 180 qualifiers, six regionals, and a March final in Coralville. The bigger change is the one after this season. If your athlete is weighing Division III programs, the 2027-28 split is worth asking about now, and if she is weighing Division I, the list of programs is longer than it was a year ago.

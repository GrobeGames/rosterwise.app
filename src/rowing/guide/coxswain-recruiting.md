The coxswain is the one person in a racing boat who doesn't row, and coxswains come up in recruiting in their own way: their own role, their own weight rules, and their own rules about which crews they may cox.

This page reports those rules as each governing body publishes them, and what a few named programs say about coxswains on their own websites. Every weight on this page is a rule set by a named body for the races it runs.

## Start here: weight rules are minimums, met with deadweight

Every body on this page sets a **minimum** weight for coxswains. Under USRowing's rules, a coxswain who weighs less than the minimum **carries deadweight** in the boat, placed as close to their torso as possible. Spreading deadweight around the boat is forbidden, and a crew whose coxswain lacks the deadweight at the finish **is not placed**. The 2026 Dad Vail Regatta adds a rule of its own: "forcing liquids to raise cox's weight is forbidden."

This page is not advice about any athlete's weight, and there is nothing here about changing one. Questions about an individual coxswain's weight, eating or health belong with **the family's own doctor** and the program's medical staff. Each body's own document is linked under Sources below.

## What a coxswain does

USRowing defines the **coxswain** as the person who **steers** the boat and is responsible for **safety, motivating the crew and race strategy**, but who does not row.

In rowing's boat symbols, **"+" means the boat carries a coxswain**: the eight (8+) has eight rowers and a coxswain, and the coxed four is written 4+. In the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals, **every championship boat carries a coxswain**, the eight and the four alike. The NCAA's participant manual for the 2024 championships described the Division III squad of 21 as "18 competitors and three spares, one of which must be a coxswain."

Our guide to [boats, seats and sides](/rowing/guide/boats-seats-and-sides/) explains the rest of the vocabulary.

## Minimum coxswain weights, body by body

There is no single "college" coxswain minimum. The figure depends on the body running the race.

| Body (document) | Men's crews | Women's crews |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **NCAA** (2026-2027 Rules Modification for Women's Rowing) | — | **121 lb** for 2026-27, raised from 110 lb |
| **IRA** ("Coxswain and LTWT weigh-in procedures," undated, read Oct. 29, 2026) | **125 lb** for heavyweight and lightweight men's crews | **121 lb** for openweight women's crews; **110 lb** for lightweight women's crews |
| **USRowing** (Rules of Rowing, 2026 Edition, Rule 4-105.1) | **120 lb** in men's events (and **120 lb** in mixed events) | **110 lb** in women's events |
| **Dad Vail Regatta** (2026 Regatta Instruction Bulletin) | **120 lb** minimum | **110 lb** minimum |
| **ACRA** (2026 Regatta Packet, in racing attire) | **125 lb** | **110 lb** |

**The NCAA's change for 2026-27.** The NCAA raised its minimum coxswain weight in women's rowing **from 110 to 121 pounds** through a modification to Rule 4-105.1.2 of the rule book it uses for rowing, approved by the NCAA Women's Rowing Committees and its Playing Rules Oversight Panel. The modification's rationale starts with health: "Increasing the minimum weight for coxswains will increase the pool of potential student-athletes who would be able to maintain coxswain weight in a healthy manner." It adds that World Rowing and some NCAA conferences have moved to 121 pounds, while USRowing has not adjusted its minimum. Which races the new minimum covers is a question to put to the program.

**Youth regattas are different.** At USRowing-run youth regattas, the Youth National Championships and the Youth Regional Championships, coxswains have **not had to weigh in or carry extra weight since 2022**.

## Weigh-ins and deadweight in practice

The bodies also publish how they check the minimum:

- **NCAA championships.** At the 2024 championships, coxswains weighed in once a day, 1 to 2 hours before their first race where weight mattered, in racing uniform and without shoes. The day's first reading on the official scale set any deadweight required. Under the 2025-26 Division I manual, a weight violation at the Division I championship puts the crew last in that race, and a second violation excludes it from the competition.
- **IRA.** The regatta keeps only a limited supply of sand, bags and tape for deadweight, and coxswains who will need extra weight are encouraged to bring their own.
- **Dad Vail Regatta.** All coxswains must weigh in.

These describe how each body enforces its minimum. They are not guidance for any athlete.

## Who may cox which crew

**Under USRowing's rules, a coxswain of either sex may cox either crew** (Rule 4-105(b)). A male coxswain may compete in women's events and a female coxswain in men's events. Coxswains are exempt from the rule that sorts events by sex, and a mixed crew's 50/50 split does not count the coxswain.

**At the NCAA championships, male coxswains are not permitted**, in all three divisions, under the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals. Races against boats with male coxswains **do not count toward NCAA rankings or selection**.

**Lightweight crews.** Under USRowing's rules, **any coxswain may cox a lightweight crew**; the lightweight weight limit does not apply to the coxswain. Our [lightweight guide](/rowing/guide/lightweight-and-openweight/) covers the rowers' limits.

## What programs say about coxswains

A few programs describe what they look for on their own athletics websites. Each is quoted as read on October 29, 2026, with the date the page gives. These describe those programs only.

- **University of San Diego (women's, Division I).** Its walk-on guide for fall 2026 invites coxswains at **5'5" or shorter** (rowers at 5'7" or taller), and states "No Rowing Experience is Required."
- **Thomas Jefferson University (women's, Division II).** Its walk-on page, last dated for fall 2025, states "we are always looking for athletes who are interested in taking command of a boat."
- **Pacific Lutheran University (women's, Division III).** Its walk-on page, posted under a 2025 web address, describes the coxswain as "a special role … ideal for smaller-bodied leaders with sharp minds." It lists no weight or experience requirement.

### Syracuse men's rowing (its team website)

Syracuse University men's rowing describes its coxswain recruiting on its own team website (footer ©2025). It looks for coxswains with "a very highly developed base set of skills (especially steering)," along with excellent communication and practice-management ability. It assesses them through interviews, recordings and their coaches' feedback, and says top candidates are "driving their team's first varsity crew." It gives no weight or size requirement.

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Which coxswain minimum applies in your races, and at which regattas?"** The NCAA, the IRA, USRowing, the Dad Vail and ACRA each set their own.
2. **"Do you recruit coxswains, find them through walk-on tryouts, or both?"** The answer tells you which door to knock on.
3. **"How do you evaluate a coxswain?"** Ask what they want to see or hear, and when.
4. **"Would I cox the women's team, the men's team, or both?"** Under the NCAA's championship rules the answer matters for women's crews.
5. **"Who on your staff handles weigh-ins and deadweight, and who handles athlete health?"** Ask directly; the answer tells you how the program approaches the minimums.

## Every coxswain is different

The rules on this page set minimums for the races each body runs, as of the documents dated above. They are not goals, they differ from body to body, and they say nothing about what is healthy for any one athlete. Read the governing body's own document, ask each program how the rules work in its races, and take any question about your athlete's weight to your family's doctor and the program's medical staff.

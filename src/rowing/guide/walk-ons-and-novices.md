Several college rowing programs say on their own websites that they take athletes who have never rowed. This page collects what named programs publish about their own walk-on and novice programs, and the NCAA rules that sit around them.

Every program detail below is quoted or reported from that program's own page, with the date the page gives. Tryouts are set by each program and change every year, so treat this as a starting point for your own questions, not a schedule.

## Walk-on and novice: what the words mean here

**Walk-on** is a general recruiting term. Our [universal guide to walk-on types](/guide/walk-on-types/) explains the differences between walk-ons, preferred walk-ons and recruited walk-ons.

**"Novice"** is a word the programs use; it does not appear in the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals or its 2026-27 Division I, II and III Manuals (more on that below). The University of Iowa describes its walk-on tryout as "aimed at athletes who have never rowed before." Bryn Mawr College answers whether experience is needed with "No! Novice Rowing is a competitive, learn as you go experience."

Some facts about how novice rowing fits the official structure:

- **The NCAA championships have no novice event.** Under the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals, Division I races the I Eights, II Eights and Fours, Division II an Eight and a Four, and Division III the I and II Eights. The word "novice" does not appear in any of the three 2025-26 championship manuals or in the 2026-27 Division I, II or III Manuals.
- **The NCAA's participation counts include novices.** The NCAA's participant figures include **novice, junior varsity and freshman squads**, plus redshirts: everyone on a roster, not only those who race.
- **Division I counts the races.** Under Bylaw 17.19.5.2, in the 2026-27 Division I Manual's section on women's rowing, a Division I **women's** rower may compete on **20** dates of competition each academic year, and that count **includes races for the varsity, junior varsity or freshman team**.
- **Novices do race outside the NCAA championships.** The Dad Vail Regatta's points tables (revised December 10, 2025) list a **Frosh/Novice 8** for both men and women.

## Walk-ons and the Division I roster limit

**The Division I women's rowing roster limit is 68.** It applies to every Division I school in a defendant conference in the House settlement, and to schools that opted in. It was adopted June 6, 2025, effective July 1, 2025. The 2026-27 Division I Manual **states no roster limit for other Division I schools**.

Where the limit applies, walk-ons are inside it:

- **There is no walk-on exception.** After the roster deadline, anyone who takes part in practice or other team activities must be on the submitted roster. The Manual's listed exceptions are designated student-athletes, injured or medically disqualified athletes, managers and similar cases. Walk-ons are not among them.
- **The roster deadline.** The Division I roster deadline is the day before the sport's first contest used for championship selection, or **Dec. 1**, whichever comes first. The Manual itself flags that **the timing of a tryout** has roster-limit consequences.

**Men's rowing is not in the table.** The Division I roster-limit table (Bylaw 17.2) lists "Women's Rowing" and has no men's rowing entry. Ask a men's program directly how it handles its roster.

**Walk-ons register too.** The NCAA Eligibility Center says **walk-ons and preferred walk-ons must also register** with it. Our [Eligibility Center guide](/guide/ncaa-eligibility-center/) covers how, and our [rowing scholarships guide](/rowing/guide/scholarships-and-the-house-settlement/) covers the House settlement.

## What programs say about their own walk-on programs

Each entry quotes or reports the program's own words from its athletics website, as read on October 29, 2026, with whatever date the page gives. They describe those programs only. Heights are given only where the program states them, and exactly as it states them.

- **University of San Diego (women's, Division I).** Its walk-on guide states "No Rowing Experience is Required." and "Fall 2026 Tryouts Start September 16th!" Walk-ons must be enrolled full time at USD. It lists rowers at 5'7" or taller and coxswains at 5'5" or shorter.
- **Clemson University (women's, Division I).** Its walk-on FAQ, published August 13, 2026, states "No previous rowing experience is necessary for walk-on athletes." Tryouts "typically take place over a two-week period beginning in early September." The page says Clemson is generally looking for women roughly 5'8" or taller, and calls that a preference, not a requirement.
- **University of Iowa (women's, Division I).** Its walk-on tryout is "aimed at athletes who have never rowed before." Tryouts "begin within the first two weeks of classes" and include "a variety of strength and fitness measures." The page shows no year.
- **University of Virginia (women's, Division I).** It states "We will have tryouts at the beginning of the academic year and encourage you to reach out after you are admitted to UVA." The page says nothing about whether experience is needed.
- **Mercyhurst University (Division I).** On a page under Women's Rowing that names no gender, it states "You need absolutely NO prior rowing experience. None. If you know how to jog, you can try-out for rowing." It holds informational meetings "Every Wednesday from 3:00 p.m. – 5:00 p.m," and among traits of successful walk-ons it lists being tall, 5'8" or taller.
- **Seattle Pacific University (women's, Division II).** It states "No previous rowing experience necessary!" and "Tryouts are usually held within the first three weeks of the fall academic quarter and typically run for two weeks." It says "There's no cost to try out or participate," and notes that a walk-on who makes the team pays a one-time NCAA Eligibility Center fee, for which a waiver may be available. The page carries no date.
- **Bryn Mawr College (women's, Division III).** It describes novice rowing as "a competitive, learn as you go experience." It describes no tryout. Without giving a year, it lists a fall interest meeting and four fall novice competitions, in October and November, plus a physical (which must have taken place after February 18, 2026), an ImPACT test, a sickle-cell result and a swim test. The weekdays it gives match 2026.
- **Pacific Lutheran University (women's, Division III).** It states "No Experience? No Problem." Instead of a tryout it runs "an open, 2-week Learn-to-Row," with "4 practices/week September - November" and "5–6 practices/week February - May." Its page sits under a 2025 web address, and its Learn-to-Row date carries no year.

### From programs' own team websites

Three more programs describe their walk-on approach on team websites the program runs, rather than on the university's athletics site:

- **Marist (men's, Division I).** Its men's crew recruiting page accepts "walk-on athletes with past rowing experience AND true novices with no experience at all," says it is "actively recruiting novice walk-ons," and has a development squad. The page lists open-house dates from August 2026 to January 2027.
- **University of Washington (men's and women's, Division I).** Its "Row for Washington" page states "Whether you have rowed for years or excelled in another sport (and would like to walk-on), we want to talk to you," and calls this an "open door recruiting philosophy." It gives no tryout dates.
- **Syracuse University (men's, Division I).** By contrast, it states "We have limited roster spots, so we consider walk-ons on a case-by-case basis."

## The junior pathway, for context

Junior rowing has its own pathway, and it helps to know what it looks like, even for a family starting from zero. Here is how USRowing describes it:

- **Youth Nationals is invitation-only.** The USRowing Youth National Championships is an **invitation-only** regatta: crews **qualify by placing at a recognized qualifying regatta** (Rule 5-304(a)). In 2026, athletes qualified **through one of 11 regional regattas**.
- **The 2026 regatta.** The **31st** Youth National Championships were held **June 11–14, 2026** at Nathan Benderson Park in Sarasota, Fla. USRowing reported **nearly 4,000 athletes**, **870 entries** and **236 clubs**, with national titles decided in **38 events**.
- **A way in for beginners.** USRowing's **National Learn to Row Day** was **June 6, 2026**. It introduces beginners to rowing, with basic instruction on equipment, technique and safety, and anyone may take part, whatever their experience or fitness.

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"When are tryouts this year, how long do they run, and what do they include?"** Every program above sets its own schedule and its own measures.
2. **"Does the Division I roster limit apply to your program, and how many spots do you expect to have for walk-ons?"** Where the limit applies, walk-ons are counted inside it.
3. **"Does your novice squad race, and where?"** The NCAA championships have no novice event, so ask where the program's novices race.
4. **"What medical clearances and tests are needed before the first practice?"** Bryn Mawr's list is one example; each program has its own.
5. **"When do I need to register with the NCAA Eligibility Center, and is there a fee?"** Walk-ons must register; the program can tell you when.
6. **"How does a novice move onto the varsity in your program?"** Ask for the program's actual practice, not a general answer.

## Starting from zero is a real starting point

Several of the programs above say, in writing, that no rowing experience is needed. That doesn't make every tryout open, every roster spot available, or every program's approach the same. Read each program's own page, note its date, and ask the questions above before the season starts.

## Start here: these are rules, not targets

Weight is a sensitive subject, so here is the frame for everything on this page. It reports **what each rowing body's published rules say** about lightweight rowing, and **who sets each number**. It is not advice. There is nothing here about how to reach, keep or change a weight, and there won't be.

Any question about an individual athlete's weight, eating or health belongs with **the family's own doctor** and the program's medical staff, never with a website. If your athlete is considering lightweight rowing, read the governing body's own document (each is linked under Sources below) and take your questions to those professionals.

## Lightweight and openweight: what the words mean

In **lightweight** events, rowers must weigh in under a maximum set by the body running the race. The other category is **openweight**. The Intercollegiate Rowing Association (IRA) uses "heavyweight" for men: its constitution describes the IRA National Championship as being for **heavyweight men's, lightweight men's and lightweight women's** crews. Its weigh-in page uses "openweight" for women's crews.

USRowing, which publishes the national rule book, says **openweight is the only category raced at the junior level**, and that **lightweight is raced in college and internationally**. More on the junior side below.

## The limits, body by body

There is no single "college" lightweight limit. Each body sets its own, and some set a crew average while others don't.

| Body (document) | Lightweight men | Lightweight women |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IRA** ("Coxswain and LTWT weigh-in procedures," undated, read Oct. 29, 2026) | Individual maximum **160.0 lb**; crew average may not exceed **155.0 lb** | Maximum **130.0 lb**; **no crew average** |
| **USRowing** (Rules of Rowing, 2026 Edition, Rule 4-104.3) | No rower over **160 lb**; no crew average | No rower over **130 lb**; no crew average |
| **Dad Vail Regatta** (2026 Regatta Instruction Bulletin) | **160 lb** maximum; no average | **130 lb** maximum; no average |
| **ACRA** (2026 Regatta Packet) | No more than **160 lb**; no average | No more than **130 lb**; no average |
| **World Rowing** (international; July 9, 2024) | **72.5 kg**, with a **70 kg** crew average | **59 kg**, with a **57 kg** crew average |

Among the four U.S. bodies in the table, **only the IRA sets a crew average**, and only for men. ACRA's members are college **club** teams, plus junior and community college teams that can't enter the NCAA or IRA championships through their athletic department.

**The coxswain is not counted.** USRowing's rule states that its lightweight limits do not count the coxswain, and that **any coxswain may cox a lightweight crew**. Coxswains have a separate **minimum** weight instead: at the IRA, **125 lb** for lightweight men's crews and **110 lb** for lightweight women's crews. Our [coxswain recruiting guide](/rowing/guide/coxswain-recruiting/) covers each U.S. body's coxswain minimums.

## When lightweights weigh in

The bodies also differ on **when** lightweights are weighed, which matters for understanding a lightweight's season:

- **IRA, regular season.** Lightweight men weigh in the night before a race, and lightweight women the day before the first day of racing. The IRA requires a record of weigh-ins: **lightweight men must weigh in five times during the regular season before the Eastern Sprints**, and **a lightweight woman needs documented weigh-ins on at least five different race dates** to be IRA-eligible.
- **IRA, championships.** At the Sprints and the IRA National Championship, lightweight men's crews weigh in formally at an assigned time, and lightweight women's crews weigh in together as a **whole crew, bow to stroke**.
- **USRowing.** Lightweights weigh in **once a day**, in a weigh-in window **1 to 2 hours before** their first race.
- **Dad Vail Regatta and ACRA.** Lightweights weigh in **once for the regatta** (Dad Vail) or **once for the weekend**, in racing attire (ACRA).

These are the procedures each body publishes. They are not a guide to any athlete's preparation, which belongs with the athlete's doctor and the program's medical staff.

## Junior rowing races openweight

Two USRowing facts belong together here:

- Its **2026 rule book still defines a men's youth lightweight**: no rower over **150 lb**.
- But **since 2022, USRowing has offered no youth lightweight events** at the regattas it runs, including the **Youth National Championships** and the **Youth Regional Championships**. It still offers lightweight racing for rowers who are not youth.

So a high school rower racing at USRowing's youth championships races openweight.

## Which schools field lightweight crews

The IRA's member page lists its lightweight members in two groups. These are the IRA's own lists, as read on **October 29, 2026**; membership changes, so confirm with each school.

**Division 1 Lightweight Men:** Columbia, Cornell, Dartmouth, Georgetown, Harvard, Mercyhurst, MIT, Princeton, US Naval Academy, Penn, Yale.

**Lightweight Women:** Boston University, Georgetown, Gordon, Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Sacred Heart, Stanford, Wisconsin.

A program can recruit its lightweights separately. MIT's recruiting-forms page, for example, lists a crew questionnaire labelled **"Crew - Lightweight,"** separate from its **Heavyweight** and **Openweight** questionnaires. The forms page itself states no weight limit.

## Where lightweights race

**Women's lightweight is not an NCAA championship event.** Under the 2026-27 NCAA Division I Manual (Bylaw 17.19.4), the season for Division I women's lightweight rowing ends with the **IRA Women's Lightweight Rowing Championship**, not the NCAA championship. The IRA states that lightweight women's programs "are subject only to NCAA Regulations."

**At the IRA National Championship**, the IRA's general-information page (undated, listing dates for 2027 to 2029) gives these lightweight events:

- **Lightweight men:** Varsity 8+, 2nd Varsity 8+ and Coxed 4+.
- **Lightweight women:** Varsity 8+, Coxed 4+ and Double (2x).

**At the Eastern Sprints and the Women's Sprints**, lightweight crews race for their own team trophies: the men's **Jope Cup** for lightweight points, and the women's lightweight **Konrad Ulbrich** trophy, alongside the openweight trophy.

**At the Dad Vail Regatta**, the regatta's points tables (revised December 10, 2025) list lightweight eights for men and women and mark them as events "not held for lack of entries," without saying which year's regatta that describes.

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Are you recruiting my athlete as a lightweight or as an openweight (heavyweight) rower?"** It is the first thing to settle, because the rest of this page follows from it.
2. **"Which body's lightweight limit applies in your races, and is there a crew average?"** As the table shows, the answer differs by body.
3. **"How many documented weigh-ins does a lightweight need in your season, and who runs them?"** For lightweight women, the IRA ties eligibility to them; lightweight men must weigh in five times in the regular season before the Eastern Sprints.
4. **"Who on your staff, medical and athletic training, works with lightweight rowers on their health?"** Ask it directly; the answer tells you how the program approaches weight.
5. **"If my athlete's body changes, can they move between the lightweight and openweight squads?"** Ask before committing, not after.

## Every athlete is different

The numbers on this page are rules set by each body for the races it runs, current as of the documents dated on this page and the IRA's pages as read in October 2026. They are not goals, they are not the same everywhere, and they say nothing about what is healthy for any one athlete. Read the governing body's own document, and take every question about your athlete's weight to your family's doctor and the program's medical staff.

This page is about the "2k," and it is deliberately narrow. It says what a 2k erg score is, what we could and could not verify about how programs use it, and what to ask. It does not describe how to row the test or train for it; that belongs with your athlete's coaches.

## What a 2k erg score is

An **erg** (short for ergometer) is, in USRowing's definition, the indoor rowing machine. A **2k erg score** is an athlete's time to cover **2,000 meters** on it, written in minutes and seconds.

The distance matches the race. **The standard race distance is 2,000 meters**, according to World Rowing, and USRowing's course standard is **2,000 m** from start to finish. Under the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals, **every event at the NCAA women's championships, in all three divisions, is raced over 2,000 meters**.

## No rule-book standard

We searched these governing-body documents for a 2k erg standard for recruiting, and none of them sets one: the NCAA's 2026-27 Division I, II and III Manuals, its 2025-26 Division I, II and III rowing prechampionship manuals, the IRA's constitution (revised July 1, 2026) and USRowing's 2026 Rules of Rowing. USRowing's rule book has rules for indoor rowing races (its Article XI), but no recruiting standard.

**Where a program publishes a figure, it is that program's own.** It describes that program. It is not a rule from any of the documents above, and it does not tell you what any other program expects.

## What two program forms show

We could not read any named program's form that asks for a 2k score. Our [universal guide to recruiting questionnaires](/guide/recruiting-questionnaires/) covers how questionnaires work in general. Here is what we could check on two programs' rowing forms:

- **MIT** keeps separate crew questionnaires by weight class: its forms page lists links labelled **"Crew - Heavyweight," "Crew - Openweight"** and **"Crew - Lightweight."** We could not read the forms' own fields, so we can't say what they ask.
- **UMass Amherst's** women's walk-on form, keyed to fall 2026 for tryouts open to full-time, fully enrolled UMass students, asks for **height** and **"Prior athletic experience."** It has **no question about an erg score**, rowing experience, weight or coxing.

So whether a program wants a 2k score, when it wants it, and in what form, is a question for that program.

## What two programs publish about the 2k

Two men's programs publish 2k figures on their own team websites. They are quoted exactly below, as the pages read on October 29, 2026. As above, these are each program's own figures, not rules: no governing body we read publishes an erg standard, and each program sets its own. These figures describe **Syracuse men's rowing and Marist men's rowing only**. They are not standards for college rowing, they are not comparable with each other, and we don't average them or extend them to any other program.

### Syracuse University men's rowing

On its team website's recruiting page (footer ©2025), Syracuse lists, among athletic standards for recruits, a "2k erg score of about 6:20 or faster by start of high school senior year." It adds that "you will want to be around that goal by September 1 of your senior year of high school."

The same page states that "serious candidates for athletic scholarship usually are below 6:10 for their 2k erg score." It lists height as typically 6'3" or taller, adding "(must be capable of rowing long)."

### Marist men's rowing

On its team website's men's recruiting page (undated; it lists virtual open-house dates from August 10, 2026 to January 11, 2027), Marist describes its past recruits rather than a cutoff. It writes that the average incoming athlete has pulled "a 2K score in the range of 6:20-6:35 in their senior year of HS."

It also states that "Athletes with scores between 6:55 and 6:40 should not be surprised to receive contacts," a sentence the page continues with detail about coach contact in the winter of senior year. And: "The fastest incoming erg score in the last 5 years was 6:12." and "The slowest recruited athlete was a 6:55."

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Do you ask recruits for a 2k score? If so, by when, and how do you want it reported?"** The only reliable answer to "what do you need" is the program's own.
2. **"Is the figure you look for a cutoff, or a description of the athletes you've recruited before?"** A program describing past recruits is telling you something different from one setting a minimum.
3. **"Do walk-ons need a 2k score to try out?"** Walk-on forms and recruit questionnaires can ask for different things.
4. **"What else do you look at besides the erg?"** Let the coach tell you, rather than assuming.
5. **"For a coxswain, what takes the place of an erg score in your evaluation?"** Ask how the program evaluates coxswains; our [coxswain recruiting guide](/rowing/guide/coxswain-recruiting/) covers what we could verify.

## One number, many readings

A 2k score is a single, concrete number. None of the governing documents we searched sets a recruiting standard for it, and we won't invent one. When a program publishes a figure, it is describing itself. Ask each coach what the 2k means in their program, and when they want to see it.

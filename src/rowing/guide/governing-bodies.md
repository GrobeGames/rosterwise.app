College rowing has more than one governing structure. Which one a team answers to depends on whether it is a women's or men's team, openweight or lightweight, varsity or club.

This guide maps who runs what, using each organization's own documents as of October 2026. It describes the rules; the questions at the end are how you find out how they apply to a particular program.

## Women's rowing: an NCAA championship sport

The NCAA conducts the **NCAA Divisions I, II and III Women's Rowing Championships**, held on the same weekend at the same site. The next championships are in May 2027 at Nathan Benderson Park in Sarasota, Florida.

The NCAA has crowned a women's rowing champion every year since **1997**, except **2020**, when the championship was canceled for COVID-19. The Division II and Division III championships began in **2002**.

In 2025-26, the NCAA counted **148 women's rowing teams: 94 in Division I, 14 in Division II and 40 in Division III**, according to its sports sponsorship and participation data published in October 2026. How each division's championship works, and how many women row, is on our [women's college rowing](/rowing/womens/) page.

### Division II's exemption from the sponsorship minimum

Under the 2026-27 Division II Manual, a Division II championship in a women's sport requires at least **35** sponsoring schools, a minimum that took effect August 1, 2024 (Bylaw 18.4.1.2). A sport that falls below it has **three years** to recover before its championship is discontinued (Bylaw 18.4.1.3).

On January 16, 2026, Division II members adopted **Proposal No. 2026-14**, which exempts field hockey and women's rowing from the 35-school minimum. It is now Bylaw 18.4.1.2.1, effective August 1, 2026. The proposal's rationale notes that women's rowing is "conducted jointly with Divisions I and III," and its FAQ notes that if the Division II championship were discontinued, Division II programs would have no other NCAA championship to enter.

## Men's rowing: no NCAA championship

**The NCAA does not hold a men's rowing championship.** In the NCAA's 2026-27 list of current championships (Bylaw 18.3 of the Division I, II and III Manuals), rowing appears only in the women's column for Divisions I, II and III, and it is not among the 15 National Collegiate Championships. The NCAA's sports sponsorship and participation data, which covers 2000-01 through 2025-26, has no men's rowing row in any year.

The NCAA's 2026-27 limit tables don't list men's rowing either: the Division I roster-limit table names women's rowing only. Our [scholarships guide](/rowing/guide/scholarships-and-the-house-settlement/) covers what that does and doesn't tell you.

The IRA runs its own national championship for varsity heavyweight and lightweight men's crews.

## The IRA (Intercollegiate Rowing Association)

The IRA's constitution, revised July 1, 2026, gives its purpose as holding a post-season national championship each year, the **IRA National Championship**, for the top intercollegiate **heavyweight men's, lightweight men's and lightweight women's** crews. The regatta also includes a **Division 3** championship: the IRA said its 2026 regatta was "the fifth consecutive one to host a D3 championship."

**Who can join, and who can race.** IRA membership is open to four-year colleges with at least one varsity rowing team. To race at the IRA National Championship, a team must have varsity status from its school and be run by the athletics department.

**Its categories.** The IRA says its members compete at the Division 1 or Division 3 level in men's heavyweight, men's lightweight or women's lightweight rowing, and that all members may race at the IRA National Championship. As of October 29, 2026, its membership page sorts schools into four lists: Division 1 Heavyweight Men, Division 1 Lightweight Men, Division 3 Men and Lightweight Women. (The IRA writes its divisions with numerals; the NCAA uses Roman numerals.)

**Its link to NCAA rules.** The IRA says that where its rules point to NCAA legislation, it follows the rule as written in the NCAA manual, and eligibility questions go to the NCAA.

**The affiliate regattas.** In a March 2021 announcement, the IRA called three regattas its "affiliate regattas": the Men's Eastern Sprints, the Women's Eastern Sprints and the National Invitational Rowing Championship (NIRC). All three race on Lake Quinsigamond in Worcester, Massachusetts. The [men's](/rowing/mens/) and [women's](/rowing/womens/) pages have their 2027 dates.

## Lightweight women: NCAA rules, IRA championship

For NCAA Division I women's lightweight rowing, the season ends with the **IRA Women's Lightweight Rowing Championship**, not the NCAA championship (2026-27 Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.19.4). The IRA says lightweight women's programs "are subject only to NCAA Regulations."

The schools on the IRA's Lightweight Women list are named on our [women's college rowing](/rowing/womens/) page. For what "lightweight" means, see [lightweight and openweight rowing](/rowing/guide/lightweight-and-openweight/).

## The Dad Vail Regatta

The **Jefferson Dad Vail Regatta** is staged by the Dad Vail Regatta Organizing Committee. Its organizers describe it as drawing over 100 colleges and universities from the U.S. and Canada. According to the regatta's history page, the Dad Vail Rowing Association was founded at a February 1939 meeting, set up to "foster and encourage intercollegiate rowing among colleges new to the sport."

The regatta's 2026 eligibility rules separate varsity and club men. Only crews their school considers varsity may enter the **DI/DII Men's Varsity Eight**; the **Club/DIII Men's Varsity Eight** is for club crews and NCAA Division III varsity crews. Athletes must be eligible under the rules of one of the bodies the regatta names, including the NCAA, the IRA and ACRA. All must be full-time undergraduates, and graduate students with eligibility left may also race.

## ACRA: club rowing

The **American Collegiate Rowing Association (ACRA)** says in its 2026 regatta packet that its membership is made up of university and college club rowing teams, plus junior and community college teams, men's and women's, that cannot enter the NCAA or IRA championships through their athletic department. Any club program its university recognizes may enter the ACRA Championship. From 2026, programs that have not raced it before must apply.

**ACRA's own eligibility rules.** ACRA sets its own rules on who may race. Under its 2026 regatta packet:

- **Clubs with a varsity counterpart.** Where a school has both a club and a varsity team of the same gender, an athlete who races once for the varsity is ineligible for ACRA that year. A recruited athlete, meaning one given admissions support by athletics such as a pre-read, a recruiting slot or an athletic scholarship, is ineligible for the year they appear on the roster.
- **Enrollment.** Athletes must be full-time undergraduates, taking at least 12 credit hours, or graduate students with eligibility left.
- **Seasons.** For students who enroll full time from 2026-27, ACRA has adopted the NCAA's age-based "5-for-5" model, five seasons in five years, replacing "4 in 5."

The 2027 ACRA Championships are set for May 21-23, 2027 in Oak Ridge, Tennessee.

## The NAIA

As of October 2026, the NAIA says it offers **31 national championship sports**, and rowing is not one of them. That doesn't mean no NAIA school rows: Oklahoma City University, an NAIA school, fields men's rowing and is listed by the IRA as a Division 1 heavyweight member.

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Is your team a varsity team run by the athletics department, or a club?"** The answer decides which championships the team can aim for. The IRA requires varsity status and athletics-department control; ACRA's members include club teams.
2. **"Which regattas does your team aim for at the end of the season?"** The NCAA championships, the IRA, the Dad Vail and ACRA each have their own entry rules.
3. **"Which recruiting and eligibility rules does your program follow?"** This matters most on the men's side, where there is no NCAA championship. We don't state which NCAA recruiting rules reach a men's crew; the coach, or the school's compliance office, can.
4. **"If my athlete rows for the club, what happens if the varsity asks them to race?"** Under ACRA's 2026 rules, at a school with both a club and a varsity team of the same gender, one varsity race makes an athlete ineligible for ACRA that year.
5. **"Do you have a lightweight team, and where does it race?"** Lightweight women end their NCAA Division I season at an IRA championship, not the NCAA championship.

## What's settled, and what to ask

The documents settle the outline: women's rowing has NCAA championships in three divisions, men's rowing has none, and the IRA holds its national championship for varsity heavyweight and lightweight men and for lightweight women. What they don't settle for a family is how a particular team fits that outline, and on the men's side, which recruiting rules a program follows. Those answers come from the program.

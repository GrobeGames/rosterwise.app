Scholarship questions in rowing have two layers: what the governing body's rules allow, and what a particular program does within them. This guide covers the first layer, anchored to the 2026-27 NCAA Manuals as we read them in October 2026. Only a program can answer the second, so the guide ends with questions to ask.

For the cross-sport background on the settlement itself, see our guide to [the House settlement](/guide/house-settlement/).

## Division I women's rowing: a 68-athlete roster limit

Under Bylaw 17.2 of the 2026-27 NCAA Division I Manual, the **women's rowing roster limit is 68**. It binds every Division I school that belongs to a defendant conference in the House settlement or that opted in to it. The limit was adopted June 6, 2025, and took effect July 1, 2025.

A roster limit counts people, not scholarships. After the roster deadline, anyone who takes part in practice or other team activities must be on the submitted roster. The Manual lists exceptions (designated student-athletes, injured or medically disqualified athletes, managers and similar cases), and **walk-ons are not among them** (Bylaws 17.2.2 and 17.2.2.1 to 17.2.2.12).

The roster deadline is the day before the sport's first contest used for championship selection, or **December 1**, whichever comes first (Bylaw 17.2.1). The Manual flags that the timing of a tryout has roster-limit consequences (Bylaw 17.02.19). If your athlete is thinking about walking on, see our [walk-ons and novices guide](/rowing/guide/walk-ons-and-novices/).

### Designated student-athletes

The settlement's transition rule covers some athletes already in college. A **designated student-athlete** is an athlete the school attests was, or would have been, cut from its 2025-26 roster because of the new limits, and who before April 7, 2025 either was certified or on the 2024-25 squad list, or had been recruited to, or assured of, a 2025-26 roster spot (Bylaw 17.02.8).

Designated student-athletes don't count toward the roster limit while they have eligibility left (Bylaw 17.2.2.2). The NCAA's June 23, 2025 release says this lasts "for the duration of their eligibility." Because the status is tied to the 2024-25 and 2025-26 rosters, as of 2026-27 it describes athletes already in college, not high school recruits.

## Scholarships at Division I

On June 23, 2025, the NCAA announced that from July 1, 2025, Division I rules "will no longer include sport-specific scholarship limits," and that schools under the roster limits may offer scholarships to any or all of their rostered athletes.

The 2026-27 Division I Manual's financial-aid article (Article 15) contains no sport-by-sport scholarship limit; the only cap in it is a per-athlete limit (Bylaw 15.1).

**What this guide doesn't state.** We don't state which scholarship rules apply at a Division I school that is not bound by the roster limit. If a school on your list isn't, ask the coach or the school's compliance office how it handles rowing scholarships. And at any school, what the rules allow is not the same as what a program funds: how much aid a rowing program offers is a question for that program.

### Before the settlement

The last pre-settlement figure we have read in the Manual itself is in the **2021-22 Division I Manual**: Bylaw 15.5.3.1.2 capped women's rowing at **20 scholarship equivalencies**. In an equivalency sport, the limit counts the total value of athletic aid rather than the number of athletes receiving it, so a program could divide it into partial awards.

## Division II: up to 20.0 equivalencies

Under the 2026-27 Division II Manual, women's rowing may award up to **20.0 scholarship equivalencies** (Bylaw 15.4.2.1.2). As in any equivalency sport, that total can be divided into partial awards across the roster.

## Division III: no athletic scholarships

**Division III schools may not award athletic scholarships.** Bylaw 15.01.3 of the 2026-27 Division III Manual bars aid to "any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance." Any aid a Division III rower receives has to rest on something other than athletics; each school's financial aid office can explain what that includes.

## The Ivy League: need-based aid only

The Ivy League's prospective-athlete page says: "There are no academic or athletic scholarships in the Ivy League." Ivy schools give financial aid, including to athletes, **only on the basis of financial need**, as each school's financial aid office determines.

A coach may ask for a **financial aid estimate** for a recruit, but only the financial aid office can set the aid package. A recruit may share an estimate with other Ivy schools. This applies to men's and women's rowers alike.

## Men's rowing: ask each program

The 2026-27 NCAA Manuals don't list men's rowing in their limit tables:

- **Division I:** the roster-limit table (Bylaw 17.2) lists "Women's Rowing" and no men's rowing. Article 17 has a women's rowing section (17.19) and none for men.
- **Division II:** the men's scholarship table (Bylaw 15.4.2.1.1) has no rowing entry.
- **Division III:** the no-athletic-aid rule (Bylaw 15.01.3) covers "any student."

The first two are absences in the tables, not rules that say what applies to a men's crew. So for a men's recruit at a Division I or Division II school, ask the program whether it offers athletic aid in rowing, how much, and under which rules. At a Division III school or in the Ivy League, the rules above apply to men's rowers as they do to everyone else.

How men's rowing is governed, including the fact that the NCAA holds no men's rowing championship, is covered in [who governs college rowing](/rowing/guide/governing-bodies/).

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Is your school bound by the Division I roster limit for the year my athlete would enroll?"** The 68-athlete limit binds schools in the settlement's defendant conferences and schools that opted in.
2. **"How many athletes do you plan to carry, and where do walk-ons fit under the limit?"** The Manual's roster limit has no walk-on exception.
3. **"How much athletic aid does the rowing program offer, and how is it divided?"** The rules set what is allowed, not what a program funds.
4. **"Is the offer for one year or more, and what could change it?"** The rules above don't answer this; the program's offer does.
5. **"Can you request a financial aid estimate for us?"** At an Ivy League school, a coach may ask for one; only the financial aid office sets the package.
6. **"For the men's team: do you offer athletic aid, and under which rules?"** The NCAA's limit tables don't list men's rowing, so the program's answer is the one that counts.

## What's settled, and what isn't

Settled in the documents we read in October 2026: the 68-athlete Division I women's roster limit and whom it binds; Division II's 20.0 equivalencies; Division III's ban on athletic aid; and the Ivy League's need-only aid. Not settled by anything we can cite: which scholarship rules apply at a Division I school outside the roster limit, and what applies to a men's crew outside Division III and the Ivy League. For those, ask the program.

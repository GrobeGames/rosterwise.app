Women's rowing is an NCAA championship sport in all three divisions. This page covers the teams and athletes, how each division's championship works, the coxswain rules and lightweight rowing, as of October 2026.

For the recruiting calendar and scholarships, see our [recruiting timeline](/rowing/guide/recruiting-timeline/) and [scholarships guide](/rowing/guide/scholarships-and-the-house-settlement/). For how the women's and men's sides of the sport are governed, see [who governs college rowing](/rowing/guide/governing-bodies/).

## Teams and athletes

In 2025-26, the NCAA counted **148 women's rowing teams: 94 in Division I, 14 in Division II and 40 in Division III**. That season it counted **6,712 participants** in women's rowing: 5,266 in Division I, 319 in Division II and 1,127 in Division III. Both figures come from the NCAA's sports sponsorship and participation data, published in October 2026.

Two things to know when reading those numbers:

- **They count everyone on a roster.** The NCAA's participant counts include novice, junior varsity and freshman squads, plus redshirts, not only those who race.
- **They start a new series.** The NCAA changed how it counts beginning with 2025-26: only active members are included, teams are counted by sport division, and only NCAA-sponsored sports are included. Figures from 2025-26 on may not compare directly with earlier years.

## The NCAA championships

The NCAA holds its **Division I, II and III women's rowing championships on the same weekend at the same site**. The 2026 championships were raced in May 2026 at Lake Lanier Olympic Park in Gainesville, Georgia. The next are in **May 2027 at Nathan Benderson Park in Sarasota, Florida**.

The NCAA has crowned a women's rowing champion every year since 1997, except 2020, when the championship was canceled for COVID-19. The Division II and Division III championships began in 2002.

Under the NCAA's 2025-26 championship manuals, every race across the three divisions is 2,000 meters, every championship boat carries a coxswain (the eight, 8+, and the four, 4+), and there is no novice event.

**A note on dates.** We could not find the NCAA's 2026-27 championship manuals when we checked in October 2026. The field sizes, events and scoring below come from the 2025-26 manuals, which governed the 2026 championships, plus committee decisions already announced for 2027.

### Division I

**The field.** The 2026 Division I championship had a **22-team** field: **10** conference automatic qualifiers plus **12** at-large teams chosen by the NCAA Division I Rowing Committee. The ten conferences with automatic qualification for 2026 were the Atlantic 10, ACC, Big 12, Big Ten, Coastal Athletic Association, Ivy League, MAAC, Mid-American, Patriot League and West Coast Conference. At its June 15-16, 2026 meeting, the Division I Women's Rowing Oversight Committee approved automatic qualification for the same 10 conferences.

**The events.** Teams race three events, each over 2,000 meters: the **I Eights, II Eights and Fours**. Every team must field two eights and one four. Rowers may not race in two boats at the championship, and there are no pairs.

**The squad.** From the 2026 championship, the Division I championship squad rose from 25 to **28**, allowing five spare athletes. The reimbursable travel party stayed at 31.

**Scoring.** First place earns **66 points in the I Eights, 44 in the II Eights and 22 in the Fours**. Each place below drops 3, 2 and 1 point respectively, down to 3, 2 and 1 point for 22nd. A tie for a team award is broken by I Eights place.

**Selection changes for 2027.** At its June 2026 meeting, the committee changed selection in three ways:

1. It will rank the **top 25 boats** in each class (Varsity 8+, Second Varsity 8+, Varsity 4+) to pick the field. The top boat in each class earns 75, 50 and 25 points respectively, with 3, 2 and 1 point less for each place after.
2. "Late-season performance" now means the **six weeks** before selection, not five weekends.
3. Starting in **2026-27**, fall races no longer count toward the minimum needed for at-large selection.

### Division II

**The field.** No more than **6** teams: one selected from each of the 3 regions, plus 3 at-large teams.

**The events.** Two events over 2,000 meters: an **Eight and a Four**, each with a coxswain.

**The squad.** Each team brings 16 student-athletes, including 2 spares, and 5 non-athletes.

**Scoring.** Eights earn 18, 15, 12, 9, 6 and 3 points for first through sixth; Fours earn 12, 10, 8, 6, 4 and 2. A tie is broken by the Eights finish.

**Selection.** To be selected, a team needs at least **6** races against Division I, II or III varsity crews. Three of those must be in-region races against Division II crews at 2,000 meters, with both an Eight and a Four racing.

**The sponsorship exemption.** A Division II championship in a women's sport requires at least 35 sponsoring schools under the 2026-27 Division II Manual (Bylaw 18.4.1.2). On January 16, 2026, Division II members adopted an exemption for field hockey and women's rowing, now Bylaw 18.4.1.2.1, effective August 1, 2026. Our [governing bodies guide](/rowing/guide/governing-bodies/) has the details.

### Division III

**The field.** **8** teams. Four berths go to Pool A automatic qualifiers (the Liberty League, Mid-Atlantic Rowing Conference, NESCAC and NEWMAC) and four to Pools B and C. Each of the four regions must be represented.

**The events.** Two events over 2,000 meters: the **I Eight and the II Eight**.

**The squad.** 21 per team, within a travel party of 26.

**Scoring.** I Eights: 40 points for first, dropping 3 per place to 19 for 8th. II Eights: 16 for first, dropping 2 per place to 2 for 8th.

## Coxswain rules

**The 121-pound minimum.** For the 2026-27 season, the NCAA raised the minimum coxswain weight in women's rowing from **110 to 121 pounds**. The change is a rule modification (Rule 4-105.1.2) approved by the NCAA Women's Rowing Committees and the Playing Rules Oversight Panel. The modification's rationale starts with health: "Increasing the minimum weight for coxswains will increase the pool of potential student-athletes who would be able to maintain coxswain weight in a healthy manner." It adds that World Rowing and some NCAA conferences have moved to 121 pounds, while USRowing has not adjusted its minimum. The modification doesn't spell out whether the minimum applies only at the championships or in regular-season racing as well, so ask a program how it applies.

**No male coxswains.** Male coxswains are not permitted at the NCAA championships in any of the three divisions, and races against boats with male coxswains don't count toward rankings or selection, according to each division's 2025-26 championship manual. Under USRowing's 2026 Rules of Rowing, by contrast, a coxswain of either sex may cox either crew.

**Other bodies, other minimums.** Each body sets its own coxswain minimum. The IRA's weigh-in procedures, for example, set 121 lb for openweight women's crews and 110 lb for lightweight women's crews. Our [coxswain recruiting guide](/rowing/guide/coxswain-recruiting/) covers the role.

## Lightweight women

For NCAA Division I women's lightweight rowing, the season ends with the **IRA Women's Lightweight Rowing Championship**, not the NCAA championship (2026-27 Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.19.4). The IRA says lightweight women's programs "are subject only to NCAA Regulations."

**The schools.** As of October 29, 2026, the IRA's membership page lists nine schools under Lightweight Women: Boston University, Georgetown, Gordon, Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Sacred Heart, Stanford and Wisconsin.

**The events at the IRA.** Varsity 8+ (limited to 12 entries), Coxed 4+ (open to any member, one entry per school) and the Double (2x, a two-person sculling boat), limited to 12 entries.

**The weight limit.** The IRA sets a maximum of **130.0 lb** for lightweight women, with no crew average. Other bodies set their own limits; see our guide to [lightweight and openweight rowing](/rowing/guide/lightweight-and-openweight/).

**The Women's Sprints.** The Women's Eastern Sprints is one of three regattas the IRA called its "affiliate regattas" in a March 2021 announcement. The IRA's page for it describes an invitational regatta; the 52nd is set for Sunday, May 2, 2027 on Lake Quinsigamond in Worcester, Massachusetts, with openweight and lightweight team trophies.

## Club rowing

The American Collegiate Rowing Association (ACRA) says in its 2026 regatta packet that its membership is made up of university and college club rowing teams, plus junior and community college teams, men's and women's, that cannot enter the NCAA or IRA championships through their athletic department. Its eligibility rules are in our [governing bodies guide](/rowing/guide/governing-bodies/).

## Roster limits and scholarships

Under Bylaw 17.2 of the 2026-27 Division I Manual, the **women's rowing roster limit is 68** at every Division I school that belongs to a House settlement defendant conference or opted in to the settlement. Division II allows up to **20.0 scholarship equivalencies** in women's rowing (2026-27 Division II Manual, Bylaw 15.4.2.1.2), and Division III schools may not award athletic scholarships (2026-27 Division III Manual, Bylaw 15.01.3). What that means, and what to ask, is in our [scholarships guide](/rowing/guide/scholarships-and-the-house-settlement/).

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"Is your program bound by the 68-athlete roster limit, and how many athletes do you plan to carry?"** The limit binds schools in the settlement's defendant conferences and schools that opted in.
2. **"Do you have a novice or freshman squad?"** The NCAA championships have no novice event, but the NCAA's participant counts include novice, junior varsity and freshman squads.
3. **"Which races count toward your team's NCAA selection?"** Starting in 2026-27, fall races no longer count toward the minimum needed for Division I at-large selection, and Division II has its own race requirements.
4. **"How does the 121-pound coxswain minimum apply in your regular-season racing?"** The NCAA's modification doesn't say.
5. **"Do you have a lightweight team, and does it race at the IRA?"** Lightweight women end their Division I season at the IRA, not the NCAA championship.

## What families can count on

The structure here is set out in the NCAA's and the IRA's own documents, dated above. What it can't tell you is where a particular athlete fits on a particular team: which boat, which squad, which season. That comes from the conversation with each coach.

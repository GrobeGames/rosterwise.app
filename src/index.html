Where does your athlete actually fit in college sports?

Every recruiting family hits the same wall. Your son or daughter has talent, drive, and dreams of playing college sports — but when you sit down to figure out which programs to target, the process feels impossible.

There are more than 2,200 college soccer programs alone across NCAA D1, D2, D3, and NAIA. Each one has a roster with different position needs, class-year openings, height profiles, international mixes, and recruiting pathways. Multiply that by volleyball, lacrosse, wrestling, softball, and other sports, and the scale of college recruiting research becomes overwhelming.

Most families do what they can: build a spreadsheet, visit school websites one by one, attend showcases, and hope they're finding the right college sports programs for their athlete. But none of that answers the question that actually matters: where does my athlete fit?

What college sports roster intelligence really means

Roster intelligence is the systematic analysis of every roster at every program — not just who's on the team, but what the roster tells you about opportunity. When you look at a program through this lens, you're asking deeper questions: position depth and class-year gaps, height and physical profiles, international vs. domestic composition, recruiting pathway patterns, coach tenure and program stability, transfer-portal activity, academic fit, and personalized fit scoring.

Questions

What recruiting families ask most

Recruiting timelines vary by sport, division, and individual circumstance — there is no single answer. For NCAA D1 soccer and volleyball, coaches generally cannot initiate off-campus contact or phone calls until June 15 after an athlete's sophomore year. However, many families begin researching programs, attending camps, and building recruiting lists well before that. D2, D3, and NAIA programs often have different — and sometimes more flexible — timelines. Every athlete's journey is different, and families should stay flexible as they navigate the process.

Roster analysis — or roster intelligence — is the practice of examining every player on a college sports roster to understand a program's structure, needs, and recruiting patterns. It goes beyond listing school names to reveal position depth, class-year gaps, height profiles, international composition, and recruiting pathways. This analysis helps families identify where their athlete has real opportunity, not just a name on a list.

Start by researching programs across multiple divisions — D1, D2, D3, and NAIA all offer meaningful competitive experiences. Evaluate fit across athletic, academic, financial, and cultural dimensions. Look at roster composition to understand where your athlete's position, size, and background align with a program's needs. Attend camps and showcases strategically. And remember: every recruit's journey is different, and the best-fit program may not be the one with the biggest name. Explore our recruiting guides for more detail.

NCAA Division I programs generally have the largest athletic budgets, most scholarships, and highest competitive intensity. Division II balances athletics and academics with partial scholarships. Division III does not offer athletic scholarships but provides competitive athletics within an academics-first model. NAIA programs are typically smaller institutions that can offer athletic scholarships and often have more flexible recruiting rules. Each division offers legitimate competitive experiences — the right fit depends on your athlete's goals, abilities, and priorities. Read our full guide to division differences.

The NCAA eliminated the National Letter of Intent (NLI) in October 2024. It was replaced by the Written Offer of Athletics Aid, which serves a similar function — formalizing a school's commitment to provide financial aid for athletics — but with some structural differences in how commitments work. Families navigating the recruiting process should be aware that the terminology and process have changed from what older guides and resources may describe. See our commitment guide for more detail.

The House v. NCAA settlement, approved June 6, 2025, introduced significant changes to college athletics. For recruiting families, the most relevant changes include roster limits at opt-in D1 schools and the elimination of sport-specific scholarship caps at those schools. These changes affect roster construction, scholarship distribution, and competitive dynamics. The full impact is still developing, and families should expect ongoing adjustments as schools adapt.

My RosterFit™ is a personalized score that compares your athlete's profile — position, height, graduation year, club background, GPA, and test scores — against the roster data at every college program. It combines athletic fit (how your athlete compares to current roster players at their position) with academic fit (how your profile aligns with the institution's academic profile) into a single, comparable metric. It's not a guarantee of admission or a roster spot — it's a data-informed starting point for building a smarter recruiting list. Learn how it works.

Going to a college's softball camp and being recruited at it are two different things, and Division I's rules keep them apart until a fixed moment in junior year. This page covers what those rules say, so you can decide which camps fit your plans and know what to expect when you get there. It covers Division I; if a camp is run by a Division II, Division III, NAIA or NJCAA program, ask that school what its rules allow.

## The rule: no recruiting conversations before 1 p.m. Central, September 1 of junior year

Bylaw **13.12.1.5.4** of the 2026-27 Division I Manual is the softball rule. At a school's own camp or clinic, its coach or off-campus recruiter may not have a recruiting conversation with a participant before **1 p.m. Central time on September 1 at the beginning of her junior year** of high school. The bylaw adds that this holds even at a camp that "involves only individuals who are not yet prospective student-athletes."

Three things follow from that wording:

- **It covers the school's own coach and its off-campus recruiters**, at the school's own camp or clinic.
- **It covers camps for younger players too.** A camp for freshmen and sophomores is not an exception.
- **The moment is the same as every other Division I softball first-contact rule.** Calls, messages, off-campus contact and visits all open at 1 p.m. Central on September 1 of junior year; see [the recruiting timeline](/softball/guide/recruiting-timeline/). The time was added by Proposal 2026-16, adopted June 24, 2026.

Before that moment, the camp's coaches still run the camp. The Manual says the interaction between campers and the coaches employed at a camp is generally not subject to the recruiting calendar. What waits until junior year is the recruiting conversation.

## Rules every Division I camp follows

**Open to anyone.** A Division I school's camp must be open to any and all entrants, limited only by number, age, grade level and/or gender (Bylaw 13.12.1.3). A camp can't be invitation-only.

**No recruiting by the camp's staff while it runs.** A school's staff member working a camp may not recruit any prospect from the time she reports to the camp until all camp activities end, and that includes extending a written offer of aid (Bylaw 13.12.1.5). Coaches who attend only as observers must follow the recruiting calendar's contact and evaluation periods. Written offers of aid can't be made before August 1 of senior year in any case (Bylaw 13.9.3.1).

**No camps in dead periods or recruiting shutdowns** (Bylaw 13.12.1.5).

## Where Division I softball coaches may work camps

Not every camp is run by a college. Division I softball has its own rules on when its coaches may work other camps:

- **Privately owned and other non-college camps:** a Division I softball coach may work one only while the calendar allows evaluation at **non-scholastic** events, such as travel ball, and only if the camp runs under the same rules as a school's camp: open to all entrants, publicly advertised at least **14 days** ahead, and with no free or reduced admission for award winners (Bylaw 13.12.2.2.8).
- **The rest of the year:** a Division I softball coach may work only at college camps held on a college campus or at a facility a college regularly uses for practice or games (Bylaw 13.12.1.2.4).

In 2026-27 the calendar's non-scholastic evaluation dates are six fall weekends (Oct. 17–18, Oct. 24–25, Oct. 31–Nov. 1, Nov. 7–8, Nov. 14–15 and Nov. 21–22, 2026). The calendar's camp note cites an older bylaw number for this rule; the 2026-27 Manual numbers it 13.12.2.2.8. More on travel-ball dates in [Travel Ball and College Softball Recruiting](/softball/guide/travel-ball-and-college-recruiting/).

## Before you register: questions to ask

1. **"Which coaches from your program will be working the camp?"** A camp is run under the school's name; ask who will actually be on the field.
2. **"What grade levels is this session for?"** Before junior year, a camp is a chance to be coached and seen, not to talk recruiting. Know which side of 1 p.m. Central on September 1 of junior year your daughter is on.
3. **"How many campers will there be per coach?"** It tells you how much individual attention to expect.
4. **"Is my daughter's position a need for your program?"** After the contact date, a coach can answer. Before it, the roster can: look at how many players the program lists at her position and when they graduate. See [Building Your List](/guide/building-your-list/).

Cost, travel and how many camps to attend are your family's call. We don't have figures on what camps cost or how coaches use them, and we won't estimate.

College softball has its own recruiting clock, and in June 2026 Division I set it to the minute. This page gives the rules as the governing bodies wrote them for 2026-27, with the bylaw or document for each, so you can check any of them yourself.

Rules tell you when a coach *may* reach out, not when one *will*. Use the dates below to know what a quiet phone means and what it doesn't.

## The Division I rule: 1 p.m. Central, September 1 of junior year

A Division I softball coach may not call, message or meet a recruit off campus before **1 p.m. Central time on September 1 at the beginning of her junior year of high school**. The 2026-27 Division I Manual applies that one moment to each part of recruiting:

| What a Division I softball coach may not do before 1 p.m. Central, Sept. 1 of junior year | Bylaw |
|---|---|
| Call the recruit or her family | 13.1.3.1.4 |
| Take a call from the recruit or her family | 13.1.3.2.8 |
| Send recruiting materials, texts, email or other electronic messages | 13.4.1.4 |
| Make in-person recruiting contact off campus | 13.1.1.1.2 |
| Pay for an official visit | 13.6.2.1.4 |
| Host an unofficial visit with athletics involvement (meeting athletics staff, an athletics-specific tour, free tickets) | 13.7.1.4 |
| Have a recruiting conversation with her at the school's own camp or clinic | 13.12.1.5.4 |

After that moment, calls are at the school's discretion (Bylaw 13.1.3.1.4). A recruit at a school on a non-traditional calendar, such as one in the Southern Hemisphere, uses **1 p.m. Central on the opening day of classes of her junior year** instead.

Softball is an exception to Division I's general rule. Under that rule, calls and electronic messages may start **June 15 after sophomore year** and off-campus contact **August 1 of junior year** (Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.4.1 and 13.1.1.1). Advice that quotes June 15 or August 1 for "all sports" doesn't apply to Division I softball.

## Where the time came from: Proposal 2026-16

Until June 2026 the softball provisions said **September 1** at the beginning of junior year, with no time of day. **NCAA Division I Proposal 2026-16**, sponsored by the Division I Softball Oversight Committee, added "1 p.m. Central time" to each of them. It was **adopted final on June 24, 2026** and took effect immediately.

The committee's stated reason was that a single start time "prohibits disruptive recruiting activities during the overnight hours." In practice, no Division I softball call may come before 1 p.m. Central that day.

One detail if you look the rule up yourself: the proposal's text numbers some provisions differently from the 2026-27 Manual, because other legislation adopted the same summer renumbered them. This page cites the Manual's numbers.

## What the time means where you live

On September 1, **1 p.m. Central** is **2 p.m. Eastern** and **11 a.m. Pacific**.

The students who started junior year in fall 2026, the high-school **class of 2028**, reached the moment on **September 1, 2026**. The **class of 2029** reaches it on **September 1, 2027**.

## What a recruit can do before then

The rules above are written about what a college's staff may do. Your daughter can still email a coach, fill out a program's [recruiting questionnaire](/guide/recruiting-questionnaires/), share video and go to camps. What a Division I coach can't do before the date is answer with recruiting messages or take her call. Silence from a Division I softball coach before 1 p.m. Central on September 1 of junior year is the rule working, not a verdict on your athlete. For how to write a first email, see [Contacting College Coaches](/guide/contacting-coaches/).

## The 2026-27 Division I softball recruiting calendar

Softball has its own Division I recruiting calendar. The NCAA's 2026-27 edition (updated July 30, 2026) sets these periods:

| Dates | Period | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| Aug. 1–9, 2026 | Contact | In-person contact and evaluation off campus |
| Aug. 10–Nov. 22, 2026 | Evaluation | High-school practices and games only |
| Aug. 28–Sept. 3, 2026 | Dead | No in-person contact or evaluation, no visits |
| Oct. 17–18, Oct. 24–25, Oct. 31–Nov. 1, Nov. 7–8, Nov. 14–15, Nov. 21–22, 2026 | Evaluation | High-school **and travel-ball** practices and games |
| Nov. 9–12, 2026 | Dead | As above |
| Nov. 23, 2026–Jan. 2, 2027 | Quiet | In-person contact only on the college's campus |
| Nov. 25–29, Dec. 22–26, Dec. 31, 2026–Jan. 2, 2027 | Recruiting shutdown | No recruiting of any kind, calls and messages included |
| Dec. 9–12, 2026 | Dead | As above |
| Jan. 3–May 31, 2027 | Evaluation | High-school practices and games only |
| June 1–11, 2027 | Dead | Moves with the Women's College World Series |
| June 12–July 31, 2027 | Contact | In-person contact and evaluation off campus; the start moves with the World Series |

Coaches may also evaluate at state high-school regional and championship games that don't fall in a dead period. The 2027 Women's College World Series is scheduled for **June 3–11, 2027**; the calendar says that if it runs late, the dead period runs through the final game.

The NCAA's own definitions: in a **contact period**, coaches may make in-person, off-campus recruiting contacts and evaluations. In an **evaluation period** they may watch and assess, but not make in-person contact off campus. In a **quiet period**, in-person contact happens only on the college's campus. A **dead period** allows no in-person contact or evaluation and no visits. A **recruiting shutdown** allows no recruiting at all: no contacts, evaluations, visits, correspondence or phone calls.

Two Division I limits sit on top of the calendar. Each school gets **seven recruiting opportunities** with a recruit per year, contacts and evaluations combined, and from 1 p.m. Central on September 1 of junior year **no more than three** may be off-campus contacts. Contacts during an official or unofficial visit don't count against the seven (Bylaw 13.1.5.9).

## Signing day: November 11, 2026

For Division I and Division II softball, signing opens at **7 a.m. on the second Wednesday in November: November 11, 2026**. There is no early or late period for softball; each school sets its own last day (Division I Bylaw 13.02.13.1(f) and Figure 13-2; Division II Bylaw 13.02.10.1(c)).

**Division I no longer uses the National Letter of Intent.** The NCAA eliminated it in 2024, and a recruit who receives a Division I or II athletics scholarship signs the school's financial aid agreement instead. A Division I school may put an offer of aid in writing from **August 1 of senior year**, and not before (Bylaw 13.9.3.1). For what a verbal offer before then does and doesn't mean, see [Verbal Commitment vs. NLI vs. Written Offer of Athletics Aid](/guide/verbal-commitment-vs-nli/).

## Division II, Division III, the NAIA and the NJCAA

The 1 p.m. Central rule is Division I's. The other levels set different limits, and none of them uses softball-specific dates:

**NCAA Division II.** Coaches may call, text and email **at any time** (Bylaw 13.1.3.1). In-person, off-campus contact and the first official visit wait until **June 15 before junior year** (Bylaws 13.1.1.1 and 13.3.1.2). The 2026-27 Division II calendar's one restriction for softball is a signing dead period from **7 a.m. November 9 to 7 a.m. November 11, 2026**.

**NCAA Division III.** There is **no restriction on the timing of calls, texts or email** (Bylaw 13.02.10.1). In-person, off-campus contact may begin once a recruit **has completed sophomore year** (Bylaw 13.1.1.1), and a school may pay for **one** official visit, not before **January 1 of junior year** (Bylaws 13.6.1.1 and 13.6.1.1.1). Division III gives no athletic scholarships and may not use a letter of intent; once a recruit has been accepted for admission, a school may offer only a nonbinding celebratory signing form (Bylaws 13.9.1 and 13.9.1.1).

**NAIA.** The NAIA Handbook sets no national first-contact date. It requires recruiting to follow each school's own accepted practices and, where a conference exists, the conference's recruiting rules. Ask each NAIA program what applies. See [NAIA Softball for Recruits](/softball/guide/naia-softball/).

**NJCAA.** A recruit may take an official visit to an NJCAA college from **June 1 before her junior year**: one paid visit per college per recruiting year, up to 48 hours. An NJCAA Letter of Intent may be signed only after she **has completed her junior year**. See [Junior College Softball](/softball/guide/junior-college/).

## Questions to ask coaches

1. **"When did your staff start recruiting my daughter's class, and how did you first see her?"** The answer tells you whether she's on a list from a fall travel-ball weekend, a high-school game or a camp.
2. **"Is this an offer you will put in writing on August 1 of her senior year?"** In Division I, nothing is in writing before then. Ask what the offer covers and for how long.
3. **"Which division does this team compete in, and which recruiting rules apply to it?"** The rules above differ by level, and the NJCAA assigns divisions by sport, not by college.
4. **"What happens between now and signing day?"** Ask about visits, the dead and quiet periods ahead, and what the coach needs from you before November.

## Every timeline is different

The date opens the door; it doesn't decide anything. When a recruit hears from coaches, and from which ones, varies, and we don't have data on how common each path is, so we won't guess. What the rules do settle is when a Division I coach may speak, and that is 1 p.m. Central on September 1 of junior year.

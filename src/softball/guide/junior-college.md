The National Junior College Athletic Association (NJCAA) governs athletics at its member two-year colleges and runs softball in three divisions. This page covers the NJCAA's softball rules for 2026-27 from its own Bylaws and Sport Codes. For how the junior-college route works in every sport, see [NJCAA and Junior College Athletics Explained](/guide/njcaa-juco-explained/).

## NJCAA softball in 2026-27

The NJCAA's own 2026-27 softball roll, read October 29, 2026, lists **360 teams**:

| NJCAA division | Teams |
|---|---|
| Division I | **136** |
| Division II | **160** |
| Division III | **60** |
| No division shown on the roll | **4** |
| **Total** | **360** |

The roll places them in **24 regions**. The NJCAA's planning document for its 2028 postseason counts 140 Division I and 166 Division II softball teams without saying when it counted them, so we use the 2026-27 roll.

**The division belongs to the sport.** The NJCAA's Bylaws say divisional play is designated by sport, not by school or athletic department. A college's softball team can play in a different division from its other teams, so check the softball team's own division.

## What each division may pay

The division decides what a softball scholarship can cover (NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27, Article VI):

| | May cover |
|---|---|
| **Division I** | Tuition and course fees; room and board; course-related books; up to **$250** a year of required course supplies; **one** round trip home a year |
| **Division II** | Tuition and course-related fees; course-related books; up to **$250** a year of required course supplies. **No** travel costs |
| **Division III** | **No athletic scholarships of any kind** |

**How many scholarships a team may give** is listed on the NJCAA's Sports Procedures Chart, which we have not been able to read. We don't print a number. Ask each coach how many the team gives and what each one covers.

## Letters of intent and visits

**The NJCAA Letter of Intent.** A recruit can sign one only after she **has completed her junior year of high school**, for the following year. The earliest signing date for each sport is on the Sports Procedures Chart.

**Official visits.** A recruit may take an official (paid) visit to an NJCAA college from **June 1 before her junior year**. A college may pay for **one** official visit per recruit per recruiting year, lasting no more than **48 hours**, and may cover the recruit and **two guests**.

## Two seasons

An NJCAA athlete may play a maximum of **two seasons** in a sport, at any intercollegiate level (varsity, JV or club), however long she attends college. Playing any part of an official NJCAA game during the year counts.

## The 2027 NJCAA World Series, and what changes in 2028

| Division | 2027 World Series |
|---|---|
| Division I | **May 24–29, 2027**, Choccolocco Park, Oxford, Ala. **20 teams**: 16 district representatives and 4 at-large |
| Division II | Choccolocco Park, Oxford, Ala. **20 teams**: 16 district representatives and 4 at-large |
| Division III | **May 26–29, 2027**, Carrier Park, East Syracuse, N.Y. |

From **spring 2028**, Divisions I and II each move to a **32-team** postseason: eight four-team super regionals, then the World Series.

We haven't printed Division II's 2027 dates: the NJCAA's Sport Codes give two that don't agree, and we'll add them once the NJCAA settles them.

## Moving on to a four-year school

If your daughter is weighing a junior college first and a four-year school after, the rules for that move depend on the four-year school's division and on her academic record. Start with our general guide, [NJCAA and Junior College Athletics Explained](/guide/njcaa-juco-explained/), and the NCAA's own *Guide for Two-Year Transfers*.

One softball rule to know: a softball player who **transfers from a two-year college to an NCAA Division I school and enrolls after that school's first term can't compete until the next academic year** (2026-27 Division I Manual, Bylaws 14.5.4.1.1 and 14.5.4.2.4). The NCAA's two-year guide says the same: such a player "may not compete until the fall term."

## Two-year colleges outside the NJCAA

California's community colleges play softball in the **CCCAA** (also called the 3C2A), and two-year colleges in the Northwest play in the **NWAC**. Both run their own softball championships, outside the NJCAA. They're not in the counts on this page; ask a CCCAA or NWAC college about its own rules.

## Questions to ask junior-college coaches

1. **"Which NJCAA division is your softball team in?"** It decides what a scholarship can cover.
2. **"How many scholarships do you give, and what does each one cover?"** The per-team number is on the NJCAA's chart, not in its Bylaws.
3. **"Where have your players gone after two years?"** Ask for names you can look up on the four-year schools' own rosters.
4. **"What do I need academically to transfer to the level I'm aiming for?"** The four-year school's division sets the academic rules for the move.

## Q1. Appeal status and litigation since 2026-08-03 (10th Circuit, district court, MDL, related suits)

### Takeaway
The NCAA appealed on **Aug. 2, 2026**. On **Aug. 21, 2026** a 2-1 Tenth Circuit panel **stayed** the July 31 preliminary injunction until the appeal is decided, which put the age-based rules back in force for the class. ## Q2. Division II: did the Executive Board adopt the model?

### Takeaway
**Yes.** NCAA.org reported on **Aug. 5, 2026** that the DII Executive Board adopted emergency legislation for the age-based model, "effective immediately for the 2026-27 academic year." The release does not give the Board's vote date. The NCAA's Eligibility 101 page now covers "Division I and II."

### Cited Findings
- "Division II adopts age-based eligibility model." Published Aug. 5, 2026, byline Asha Evans.
  - The Executive Board adopted emergency legislation after the Management Council's July recommendation, effective immediately for 2026-27.
  - Clock start: the earlier of full-time enrollment at any college or university (domestic or international, including junior colleges) or the start of the regular academic year after the 19th birthday.
  - Replaces four seasons within 10 semesters/15 quarters.
  - Exceptions: pregnancy, active-duty military service, official religious missions, administered by the Eligibility Center.
  - Transition:
    - athletes with no eligibility left under the old rules get none;
    - for current athletes, campus compliance staff determine which model is more favorable;
    - 2026-27 prospects are evaluated by the Eligibility Center under the more favorable model;
    - fall 2027+ initial enrollees are under the age-based model only.
  - Joint Executive Board/Management Council statement (no individual named): the change "should apply going forward, and not retroactively."
  - Source: [NCAA.org](https://www.ncaa.org/news/division-ii-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model/) (T1)
- "DII Management Council recommends emergency legislation to adopt age-based eligibility model for 2026-27." Published July 21, 2026 (Asha Evans).
  - The Council acted "this week"; the release gives no exact date.
  - Quote from Ben Cherry (FAR, Cal State San Marcos; Council chair): "This age-based model simplifies the rules structure by providing clarity and consistency."
  - Source: [NCAA.org](https://www.ncaa.org/dii-management-council-recommends-emergency-legislation-to-adopt-age-based-eligibility-model-for-2026-27/) (T1)
- Eligibility 101 page, titled "NCAA Division I and II Age-Based Eligibility Rules: Eligibility 101." Lists eliminated rules, including "10 semesters/15 quarters of full-time eligibility (DII only)." No date stamp. — [NCAA.org Eligibility Center](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules/) (T1)

### Inferences
- The page's "We could not verify that final adoption had occurred" is now resolved: adoption is confirmed by a T1 source dated Aug. 5, 2026, two days after the page's verification date.

### Gaps
- The exact date of the Executive Board vote is not stated in the release. The LSDBi entry for the emergency legislation was not located.
- One search result claimed DII also adopted a blanket athletics-aid waiver for 2027-28. The release I opened does not mention it, so it is **not** reported here.

## Q5. NCAA rule text and guidance: confirm or correct each rule detail

### Takeaway
The NCAA's current guidance page is "NCAA Division I **and II** Age-Based Eligibility Rules: Eligibility 101." It confirms the page's core rule details, with three refinements:
- the "first full-time enrollment" trigger explicitly includes two-year and international institutions;
- the service exception covers "a similar service commitment," not only military and missions;
- the pregnancy exception pauses the clock only for the actual period the athlete cannot compete, with medical documentation required.

The NCAA itself says calling this "5 for 5" is "not accurate." I found no NCAA amendment to the DI rule between Aug. 3 and Oct. 8.

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility 101 (NCAA Eligibility Center; no date stamp; the old URL `/sports/2026/6/23/ncaa-division-i-age-based-eligibility-rules-eligibility-101.aspx` now 301-redirects):
  - "While some have referred to this as the NCAA "5 for 5" rule, that is not accurate."
  - Trigger 1: "The academic term when the student-athlete first enrolls full time and attends class at any college or university," including domestic four-year, international and two-year institutions.
  - Trigger 2: the start of the regular academic year after the 19th birthday if the athlete turns 19 before Sept. 1. "For an individual who turns 19 on or after Sept. 1, the period of eligibility begins" the following academic year unless the athlete enrolls full time earlier.
  - "It does not pause because a student-athlete does not compete …," and the clock continues through transfers, sitting out, team changes and time away.
  - Eliminated rules:
    - seasons of competition;
    - athletics redshirt rules;
    - delayed enrollment/organized competition rules;
    - sport-specific enrollment timelines;
    - academic nonqualifier season limits (DI only);
    - 10 semesters/15 quarters (DII only).
  - Eliminated waivers: medical hardship; extension of eligibility; season of competition; athletics activity; delayed enrollment/organized competition.
  - Service exception: "Time spent in active-duty military service, on an official religious mission or in a similar service commitment" can be excluded if the athlete does not take part in organized competition during that time.
  - Pregnancy exception: "A pregnant student-athlete may pause the eligibility period for the duration of the actual period" the athlete cannot compete, with medical documentation.
  - Transition table:
    - final season used in 2025-26: no additional eligibility;
    - current athletes with eligibility left: more beneficial rule;
    - 2026-27 initial enrollees: more beneficial rule;
    - fall 2027+ initial enrollees: age-based only.
  - Current athletes who stay under the previous rules lose access to the eliminated waivers.
  - "The Eligibility Center plans to have information regarding the age-based rules in student accounts in early August."
  - Source: [NCAA.org Eligibility Center](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules/) (T1)
- "Division I adopts age-based eligibility model." June 23, 2026, Meghan Durham Wright.
  - Cabinet "unanimously" approved; "not final until the Cabinet meeting concludes Wednesday" (June 24, 2026).
  - Exceptions apply only if the athlete does not take part in organized competition for the duration.
  - Waiver requests under the previous rules, for circumstances during or before 2025-26, were due by **July 31, 2026**.
  - Quotes: Josh Whitman (Illinois AD, Cabinet chair) and Charlie Baker (NCAA president).
  - Source: [NCAA.org](https://www.ncaa.org/news/division-i-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model/) (T1). Legacy URL: https://www.ncaa.org/news/2026/6/23/media-center-division-i-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model.aspx
- "DI Board of Directors directs Cabinet to advance age-based eligibility rules." April 27, 2026.
  - The concept as written then started the clock at the academic year after turning 19 **or finishing high school**, whichever came first. The adopted rule uses enrollment instead.
  - Also directed: keep the four-in-five rules for 2025-26 competitors, and no retroactivity for athletes whose eligibility ends by spring 2026.
  - Source: [NCAA.org](http://ncaa.org/media-center-di-board-of-directors-directs-cabinet-to-advance-age-based-eligibility-rules/) (T1). Legacy URL: https://ncaa.org/news/2026/4/27/media-center-di-board-of-directors-directs-cabinet-to-advance-age-based-eligibility-rules.aspx

### Inferences
- The page's rule section is substantively accurate. The refinements above should be added. The JUCO section's statement that "a junior college is a college" is now explicitly supported by the NCAA's "two-year institutions" wording.

### Gaps
- The Eligibility 101 page has an FAQ heading but no FAQ text, and no date stamp.

## Q7. Federal legislation: Protect College Sports Act (new since 2026-08-03)

### Takeaway
On **Sept. 28, 2026** the Senate passed S. 4668, the Protect College Sports Act of 2026, by **77-22**. Section 113(b) would set a statutory 5-calendar-year eligibility window for DI and DII, starting at the earlier of the academic year after the 19th birthday or initial full-time enrollment. The House received the bill on **Oct. 5, 2026**, and it is "Held at the desk." **It is not law.**

  - "Passed Senate with an amendment by Yea-Nay Vote. 77 - 22. Record Vote Number: 250" on Sept. 28, 2026.

- The bill's exception (D) would *permit* associations to adopt injury or medical pauses. The NCAA rule currently eliminates medical hardship waivers. This should be reported as a difference in text, not a prediction.

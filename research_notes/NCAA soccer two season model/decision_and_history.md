# NCAA Division I Men's Soccer Two-Semester ("Fall-Spring" / "Year-Round") Playing Season: Decision and Legislative History

All sources below were opened and read on 2026-10-08 unless a line says otherwise. Tier key, from the RosterWise content standards: **T1** = governing body or institution publishing its own fact. **T2** = established news reporting an event or a named official's quote. Search-result snippets I could not open are listed only under Gaps.

**Short version:** The unverified summary ("an NCAA committee approved soccer to become a fall and spring sport") is **only partly right and is out of date.**
1. The change covers **Division I men's soccer only**. Women's soccer is not included, and no Division II or III action was found.
2. The committee did adopt it on 2026-05-13. The DI Cabinet then **remanded** it on 2026-06-24.
3. The DI Cabinet **affirmed** it on Wednesday 2026-10-07, but by **less than 75%**. It is therefore **not yet final**: it is in a **30-day rescission period**, and DI schools and conferences can still defeat it.
4. If it survives, it takes effect **August 1, 2027** (the 2027-28 season).

## 1. What exactly was approved, by which body, when, and is it final?

### Takeaway
The DI Men's Soccer Oversight Committee adopted Proposal No. 2026-27 on 2026-05-13. The DI Cabinet remanded it on 2026-06-24 and then affirmed it on 2026-10-07, short of the 75% supermajority. Because of that, it is now in a 30-day rescission period. It becomes effective for 2027-28 unless at least two-thirds of DI members (at least 245 schools and/or conferences) vote to rescind it. As of 2026-10-08 it is **not final**.

## 2. Which divisions and genders? Any opt-in, conference-level, or pilot element?

### Takeaway
The change covers **Division I men's soccer only**. The DI Women's Soccer Oversight Committee reviewed the idea and declined to propose it. I found no DII or DIII action. There is no opt-in or pilot. Conferences do get autonomy to choose whether their conference championship is held in fall or spring.

## 3. Effective date and transition

### Takeaway
If not rescinded, the change is effective **August 1, 2027**, before the 2027-28 playing season. Nothing takes effect immediately. A new optional "7-day transition period" applies before each segment.

### Cited Findings
- Q&A Question 2: "are any aspects of this proposal effective immediately?" The answer is "No… all aspects of this proposal become effective August 1, 2027, prior to the beginning of the 2027-28 playing season" — [NCAA Q&A, Q2](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- The May release also gives "effective Aug. 1, 2027" — [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1
- The Oct. 7 article gives "starting with the 2027-28 academic year" if not rescinded — [NCAA.org, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) — T1
- **7-day transition period** (Q11 to Q20):
  - **Who:** only institutions that have not yet begun classes for the fall or spring term.
  - **Fall timing:** may begin on the **Thursday of the 14th weekend before Thanksgiving**.
  - **Spring timing:** may begin on the **Friday of the 12th weekend before the start of the NCAA DI Men's Soccer College Cup**.
  - **Activities allowed:** the same as out-of-season athletically related activities under Bylaw 17.1.7.2-(a).
  - **Optional:** it is not mandatory.
  - **Season count:** it does **not** count toward the 132-day playing season.
  - **Incoming students:** may take part if accepted for full-time enrollment and no longer enrolled at their previous school.
  - **Other effects:** it starts the 45-day temporary certification period and triggers transfer status.
  - Source: [NCAA Q&A, Q11-Q20](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1

### Inferences
- Under the new model, the 2027 fall segment would open in August 2027, and the first spring segment and spring College Cup would fall in **spring 2028**. NCAA has not announced the championship dates.

## 4. What the new model looks like concretely

### Takeaway
The model keeps the **132-day playing season** and the **25-contest maximum**, and splits them into two segments:
- **Fall:** up to 18 contests (15 regular-season plus 3 scrimmages/exhibitions; minimum 8 regular-season), from late August to just before Thanksgiving.
- **Spring:** up to 10 contests (8 regular-season plus 2 scrimmages/exhibitions; minimum 3 regular-season), starting mid-February.

The College Cup moves to spring. The transfer window becomes a single 15-day window that opens after the spring championship.

### Cited Findings
**Season length and contest cap**
- The model keeps the current 132-day playing season (Q4: "No. If adopted, this proposal would maintain the current 132-day playing season limit") and the 25-contest maximum, which includes regular-season games and scrimmages/exhibitions (Q6) — [NCAA Q&A, Q4 and Q6](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
  - This T1 source confirms that the **current** DI men's soccer playing season is 132 days and the current maximum is 25 contests.
- **Bylaw number correction:** the Q&A cites the current soccer playing-season bylaws as **Bylaw 17.22** ("Soccer"), including:
  - 17.22.1 Length of Playing Season
  - 17.22.2 Preseason Practice
  - 17.22.3 First Contest
  - 17.22.5 Number of Contests
  - 17.22.6 Out-of-Season Activities

  The brief's "Bylaw 17.29" is **not** the soccer bylaw in the current DI Manual. Historically, soccer sat at 17.19 in 2015 and 17.20 in 2019. Sources: [NCAA Q&A, references](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1; [LSDBi Proposal 2019-90 (amends 17.20)](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=104160) — T1; [LSDBi Proposal 2015-82 (Bylaw 17.19 soccer)](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=3260) — T1

**Fall segment**
- Contest limits: maximum **18 contests** (15 regular-season plus 3 scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum **8 regular-season contests** — [NCAA Q&A, Q6](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- Dates: "starting in late August and going until the **Saturday before Thanksgiving**" — [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1
  - **Discrepancy:** the Oct. 7 article says "starting in late August and concluding the **week before Thanksgiving**" — [NCAA.org, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) — T1. The two are not necessarily in conflict ("week before" is looser), but use "the Saturday before Thanksgiving" only with the May source. The Q&A gives no fall end date.

**Spring segment**
- Contest limits: maximum **10 contests** (8 regular-season plus 2 scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum **3 regular-season contests** — [NCAA Q&A, Q6](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- Dates: begins in **mid-February**, with up to 10 contests before the DI Men's Soccer Championship — [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1; [NCAA.org, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) — T1
- Scrimmages and exhibitions do **not** count toward the minimums — [NCAA Q&A, Q7](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- Note: the segment maximums add up to 28 (18 + 10), but the annual cap stays at 25. A team cannot max out both segments — [NCAA Q&A, Q6](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1 (arithmetic from the stated figures)

**Days off**
- Two additional consecutive days off in the fall segment and three in the spring segment.
- These days do not count toward the 132 days, and countable athletically related activity is prohibited on them.
- They cannot be used to meet the one-day-off-per-week rule or the 14 additional autonomy-legislation days off.
- Source: [NCAA Q&A, Q21-Q28](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1

**Championship (College Cup)**
- It moves to the spring segment (Q5) — [NCAA Q&A, Q5](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- "Specific dates for the postseason will be determined later" — [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1

**Current format (for comparison)**
- The season runs in a **10- to 13-week fall window**, with the Men's College Cup in **December** — [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1

**Eligibility details (these answer the June remand concerns)**
- **Midyear transfers:** the competition-in-year-of-transfer rule (Bylaw 14.5.5.2) would stop a player who competed for one DI school in the fall from competing for a new DI school that winter or spring — [NCAA Q&A, Q9](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1
- **Midyear initial enrollees:** they **are** eligible to compete in the spring segment if they enroll full-time, attend the first day of classes, and are otherwise eligible — [NCAA Q&A, Q10](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1

**Transfer window (companion change)**
- Starting in 2027-28: one **15-consecutive-day** notification-of-transfer window in the spring, opening the day after the DI Men's Soccer Championship.
- This replaces the current **45-day** window, which is split into two windows.
- Source: [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1
- The Cabinet remanded this change together with the season change in June — [NCAA.org, June 24, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) — T1
- The Oct. 7 article says the window "would move to after the spring championship" — [NCAA.org, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) — T1

**What it does not include**
- Unlike U.S. Soccer's October 2025 NextGen College Soccer Committee white paper, the proposal does **not** include:
  - an expansion of the playing and practice season, or
  - a national regionalized schedule with tiering or promotion/relegation.
- Source: [NCAA Q&A, Q3](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1

### Inferences

- The Oct. 7 article does not restate the 15-day figure for the transfer window. The 15-day detail rests on the May 13 release (T1). Confirm it is still in the affirmed version when the LSDBi record or an educational column appears.

## 5. Stated rationale

### Takeaway
NCAA's official rationale is student-athlete well-being:
- less time demand in any one term
- fewer midweek games and less missed class time
- more predictable schedules
- more recovery between matches (injury prevention)
- more campus integration

In October, NCAA added that supporters say the model better develops talent, gives more rest, and reduces weekday travel. Student-athlete representatives on the Cabinet were among the advocates.

### Cited Findings
- The May release lists these benefits for student-athletes:
  - a more balanced academic and athletic experience, with less time demand in any single term
  - a decompressed season with "potentially fewer midweek games," meaning less missed class time and support for academic performance and degree completion
  - more predictable weekly schedules
  - more recovery time between matches, aiding injury prevention and return-to-play protocols
  - greater integration into campus life
  - Source: [NCAA.org, May 13, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) — T1
- Oct. 7 additions:
  - Supporters say a year-round season better develops talent and allows more rest and recovery between games and less weekday travel.
  - Student-athlete representatives on the Cabinet are among the advocates.
  - Source: [NCAA.org, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) — T1
- June 24 additions:
  - The Cabinet acknowledged the aim to "modernize the calendar to grow the sport."
  - Men's soccer student-athletes "broadly support the change."
  - Source: [NCAA.org, June 24, 2026](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) — T1

## 6. History: earlier proposals and precedents (HISTORICAL — pre-2026)

### Takeaway
The direct legislative predecessor is **DI Proposal 2019-90**:
- **Sponsors:** ACC, Big Ten and Pac-12.
- **Structure:** a 132-day "Academic Year Playing and Practice Season Model" with 14 fall plus 9 spring contests (23 total).
- **Outcome:** tabled by the DI Council on April 1, 2020 because of COVID-19; carried into 2021-22; tabled again April 13, 2022 pending the Transformation Committee; "Defeated Final" on May 2, 2023 under the sunset provision. **It was never voted down on the merits.**

The 2016 coaches' campaign is documented only in news sources I could not open.

### Cited Findings
**DI Proposal 2019-90**
- **Title:** "Playing and Practice Seasons -- Men's Soccer -- Academic Year Playing and Practice Season Model"
- **Sponsors:** Atlantic Coast Conference, Big Ten Conference, Pac-12 Conference
- **Intent:** "In men's soccer, to modify the playing and practice season, as specified."
- **Bylaw:** amends Bylaw 17.20
- **Effective date:** originally Aug. 1, 2022. The sponsors changed it to **Aug. 1, 2023** on Dec. 10, 2021.
- Source: [NCAA LSDBi, Division I Proposal 2019-90](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=104160) — T1
- **Proposed structure:**
  - A **132-day** season split into fall and spring segments.
  - **Fall:** 14 contests (12 regular-season plus 2 scrimmages/exhibitions), ending the Sunday before Thanksgiving.
  - **Spring:** 9 contests (8 regular-season plus 1 scrimmage/exhibition), ending with the DI Men's Soccer Championship.
  - Edited Feb. 1, 2020 to allow one contest per weekend and limit midweek contests (two in the fall segment, no more than one in any week; one in the spring segment).
  - Source: [LSDBi 2019-90](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=104160) — T1
- **Rationale and positions:**
  - The sponsors cited injury-rate data, academic concerns, less missed class time, better weather and attendance for championships, and training closer to professional and international soccer.
  - Position statements: Student-Athlete Advisory Committee **supports**; Committee on Academics **opposes**; Legislative Committee **opposes**; CSMAS **opposes**; Student-Athlete Experience Committee **no formal position**.
  - Source: [LSDBi 2019-90](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=104160) — T1
- **Status history:**

  | Date | Status |
  |---|---|
  | Jul 15, 2019 | Submitted |
  | Feb 7, 2020 | Ready for Vote |
  | **Apr 1, 2020** | **Tabled by the Council due to COVID-19** |
  | Jul 1, 2021 | Council moved it to the 2021-22 cycle |
  | Feb 7, 2022 | Ready for Vote |
  | **Apr 13, 2022** | **Tabled pending the work of the Transformation Committee** |
  | **May 2, 2023** | **"Defeated Final," based on application of the sunset provision** |

  - No vote tallies are recorded — [LSDBi 2019-90](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=104160) — T1
- **Contemporaneous reporting (T2):**
  - Maryland coach **Sasho Cirovski** had led the campaign for seven years, with support from Stanford's **Jeremy Gunn** and formal endorsements from the Big Ten, ACC and Pac-12.
  - Games would drop from 25 to 23, the fall half would end at Thanksgiving, and the championship would move to spring, with an expected 2022 start.
  - An April 2020 vote was expected. The Council had 64 votes in total and 33 were needed to pass.
  - The Ivy League said it would vote no; the Atlantic 10 said yes.
  - The article cites a 2016 NCAA survey in which 80% of respondents preferred two semesters.
  - Quotes: Gunn, "If we can reduce the classes missed, it's a big win"; Cirovski, "Public support is critical."
  - Source: [Philadelphia Inquirer, "Historic reform plan in college soccer nears final NCAA vote after 7 years of work," Jonathan Tannenwald, Jan. 22, 2020](https://www.inquirer.com/soccer/college-soccer-reform-sasho-cirovski-maryland-jeremy-gunn-stanford-20200122.html) — T2. The survey figure is reported, not T1.

See Gaps.

**2026 relationship to U.S. Soccer**
- The NCAA Q&A confirms that U.S. Soccer's **NextGen College Soccer Committee** issued a **White Paper in October 2025** recommending a redistributed season and a spring College Cup. NCAA describes Proposal 2026-27 as "distinct and independent" of it — [NCAA Q&A, Q3](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) — T1

### Inferences
- The 2026 model is a scaled-up successor to 2019-90. It keeps the same 132 days, but allows 25 contests instead of 23 and an 18/10 segment maximum instead of 14/9. It came through the new DI governance route (sport oversight committee plus Cabinet) instead of conference sponsorship plus a Council vote. The "new governance structure" phrasing is NCAA's (May 13 release); the comparison is my own.
- Accurate shorthand: "first formally proposed in 2019 (Proposal 2019-90, sponsored by the ACC, Big Ten and Pac-12), tabled in 2020 because of COVID-19 and again in 2022, and retired under the sunset rule in 2023."

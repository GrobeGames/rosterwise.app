# NCAA soccer two-semester (fall/spring) model: documented consequences for HS recruits, current college players and families

Research date / date read for every source below: **2026-10-08**. Tier labels follow the RosterWise content standards (T1 = governing body publishing its own fact; T2 = established news, events/quotes only).

**Anchor: what was actually decided.** The premise "an NCAA committee approved soccer to become a fall and spring sport" is **partly correct and incomplete**:
- It applies to **NCAA Division I men's soccer only**. Women's soccer is not included. NCAA DII, DIII, NAIA and NJCAA have no matching announcement (see last section).
- Timeline (all T1, NCAA.org):
  1. **May 13, 2026.** The DI Men's Soccer Oversight Committee **adopted** Proposal No. 2026-27, effective **Aug. 1, 2027** (2027-28 season). The action was subject to DI Cabinet review.
  2. **June 24, 2026.** The DI Cabinet **remanded** the proposal and the related transfer-window change. Twenty DI conferences opposed it.
  3. **Oct. 7, 2026.** The DI Cabinet **"narrowly affirmed"** the proposal. It fell short of the 75% needed to skip further review, so the proposal is now in a **30-day rescission period**.
- **As of 2026-10-08 the change is NOT final.** It can still be rescinded by DI member vote.

## 1. What was decided, for whom, effective when (anchor for all implications)

### Takeaway
On Oct. 7, 2026 the DI Cabinet narrowly affirmed a **DI men's-soccer-only** move to a two-segment (fall plus spring) playing season with the **College Cup in spring**, effective **Aug. 1, 2027 (2027-28)**. It is now in a 30-day rescission period, so as of 2026-10-08 it is **not final**. Women's soccer is not changing.

### Cited Findings
- The DI Men's Soccer Oversight Committee adopted legislation to move DI men's soccer to a two-semester playing and practice season, effective Aug. 1, 2027 and beginning with the 2027-28 season. Its action was subject to DI Cabinet review at the June 23-24 meeting. — [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1, 2026-05-13)
- The proposal was introduced in January 2026 through the new DI governance structure. — [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1)
- On June 24, 2026 the DI Cabinet **remanded** the proposal to the oversight committee.
  - It noted that **20 DI conferences opposed** the proposal.
  - Their concerns: the length of the season, a lack of clarity about related rules, and the impact on championship selections, including eligibility for midseason enrollees.
  - The Cabinet separately raised concerns about the student-athlete academic experience.
  - It also remanded the accompanying men's soccer notification-of-transfer-window change.
  - Source: [NCAA-Jun24](https://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1, 2026-06-24)
- On Oct. 7, 2026 the DI Cabinet **affirmed** the proposal ("narrowly"). The vote did not reach the **75%** needed to avoid further review, so the proposal enters a **30-day rescission period**. During that period any eligible DI school or conference may vote to defeat it. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1, 2026-10-07)
- Rescission threshold wording in the release: the change takes effect if two-thirds of DI members do not rescind it, with the release citing "at least 245 schools and/or conferences." **Conflict/ambiguity flag:** the release's wording mixes "two-thirds of members" with a fixed count. Quote it exactly; don't paraphrase. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- If not rescinded, men's soccer moves to the new model starting with the 2027-28 academic year. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- In response to the remand, the committee gave the Cabinet feedback on **midyear enrollees** and **school-level scheduling challenges**. The Cabinet was "generally satisfied" that the schedule would be manageable. The release does **not** describe any specific amendment to the proposal. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- "If adopted, this proposal is specific to men's soccer and does not impact the women's soccer playing and practice season legislation." — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q1)
- The DI Women's Soccer Oversight Committee reviewed the proposal and chose not to propose a similar change for women's soccer. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- "If adopted, all aspects of this proposal become effective August 1, 2027, prior to the beginning of the 2027-28 playing season." Nothing is effective immediately. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q2)
- The proposal is distinct from the October 2025 U.S. Soccer NextGen College Soccer Committee White Paper. It does **not** include an expansion of the playing/practice season or a national regionalized schedule with tiering or promotion/relegation. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q3)
- T2 (event/quote only): AP reported the committee action on May 13, 2026. Washington coach Jamie Clark is quoted calling it "a new era for college soccer." — [AP/ESPN](https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/48761056/ncaa-mens-college-soccer-d1-schedule-change-fall-spring) (T2)

### Inferences
- The accurate shorthand is: "DI men's soccer only; affirmed by the DI Cabinet Oct. 7, 2026; subject to a 30-day rescission period; would take effect 2027-28." Calling it "soccer" without the gender and division would mislead women's-soccer families.
- **Derivation (not stated by NCAA):** the release gives no calendar deadline. Counting 30 days from Oct. 7, 2026 gives about **Nov. 6, 2026** for the end of the rescission window. Label it as a derivation if used.

### Gaps
- The exact rescission deadline date and the precise vote count/percentage at the Oct. 7 Cabinet vote are not given in NCAA-Oct7.
- The final legislative text as affirmed on Oct. 7 was not located. **Caveat:** the Q&A file's server timestamp (2026-04-03) predates the May adoption and the June remand. The NCAA has not said whether its details were amended after the remand. Re-verify the Q&A or LSDBi text once the rescission period closes.
- The legislative history is covered in depth by the parallel researcher (decision_and_history.md).

## 2. Season structure, practice, days off and missed class (what changes per NCAA's own documents)

### Takeaway
The **132-day playing season and 25-contest maximum stay the same**. They are redistributed into a fall segment (max 18 contests: 15 regular season plus 3 scrimmages/exhibitions) and a spring segment (max 10: 8 plus 2). The changes add segment minimums, a 7-day transition period before each segment, and extra required consecutive days off. The NCAA documents found do **not** change the 20/8-hour limits themselves.

### Cited Findings
- **Season length and contest limit are unchanged.** The proposal keeps the 132-day playing season and the 25-contest maximum (including scrimmages/exhibitions). It lets the championship segment span two semesters or three quarters and shifts the national championship to the spring term. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, preamble, Q4, Q6)
- **Fall segment:**
  - Max 18 contests (15 regular season plus 3 scrimmages/exhibitions).
  - Minimum 8 regular-season contests.
  - Source: [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q6)
- **Spring segment:**
  - Max 10 contests (8 regular season plus 2 scrimmages/exhibitions).
  - Minimum 3 regular-season contests.
  - Scrimmages/exhibitions do not count toward the minimums.
  - Source: [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q6-Q7)
- **Segment dates:**
  - Fall: late August through the Saturday before Thanksgiving. NCAA-Oct7 phrases the end as "the week before Thanksgiving."
  - Spring: begins mid-February and runs to the DI Men's Soccer Championship.
  - Sources: [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/); [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- **Conference championships:** their timing is not changed by the proposal. Conferences keep autonomy to hold them in the fall or spring segment. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q8)
- **7-day transition period** (optional; only for schools that have not yet begun classes for the term):
  - Fall: may begin on the Thursday of the 14th weekend before Thanksgiving.
  - Spring: may begin on the Friday of the 12th weekend before the start of the College Cup.
  - Permitted activities are the same as out-of-season athletic activities (Bylaw 17.1.7.2-(a)).
  - It does not count toward the 132 days. Any countable athletically related activity before preseason must still follow transition-period rules (day-off and hour requirements).
  - Source: [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q11-Q15)
- **Transition period and incoming players (relevant to recruits):**
  - An incoming student-athlete may take part before initial enrollment if accepted for full-time enrollment, no longer enrolled at the previous school, and otherwise eligible.
  - Participation **starts the 45-day temporary certification period**.
  - Schools may pay actual and necessary expenses.
  - Players may compete on an outside team during the transition period.
  - Participation in transition-period activities **triggers transfer status**.
  - Source: [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q16-Q20)
- **Additional required days off:**
  - Two additional consecutive days off in the fall segment and three in the spring segment. Countable athletically related activities are prohibited on those days, and they do not count toward the 132 days.
  - They may **not** be used toward the one-day-off-per-week requirement.
  - They may **not** be used toward the 14 additional required days off for schools following autonomy time-demands legislation.
  - Source: [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q21-Q24, Q26-Q27)
- Autonomy schools may place any of the 14 additional days off in vacation periods during the declared playing season, provided those days count toward the playing season. Outside the playing season, additional days off may be given only during a regular academic term while classes are in session. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q25, Q28)
- The Q&A's cited bylaws include 17.7 (Time Limits for Athletically Related Activities), 17.22 (Soccer), 17.22.1-17.22.8.1, 14.5.5.2 (Competition in Year of Transfer) and 12.1.1.1.3.1 (Temporary Certification). **Note:** soccer is Bylaw **17.22** in the current Manual per this document, not 17.29 as suggested in the assignment brief. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, references)
- **NCAA's stated rationale (claims, not measured outcomes):** a more balanced academic and athletic experience, reduced time demands in a single term, potentially fewer midweek games and less missed class time, more predictable schedules, and more recovery time. — [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1). NCAA-Oct7 adds that advocates, including student-athlete representatives on the Cabinet, argue for more rest/recovery and less weekday travel. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- **Counterpoint, also T1:** the Cabinet raised concerns about the two-semester season's effect on the student-athlete academic experience. — [NCAA-Jun24](https://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1)

### Inferences
- For families, the documented change is in **when** games happen, not **how many**: the same 25-contest and 132-day caps, split across two terms, plus a spring postseason. "Less missed class" is the NCAA's stated aim, not a documented result.

### Gaps
- No NCAA document found states any change to the CARA **20-hour/week (in-season) or 8-hour/week (out-of-season)** limits for men's soccer. The Q&A references Bylaw 17.7 but describes no change to it. Treat these as unchanged unless the final legislative text says otherwise. The final text was not located.
- No NCAA document found quantifies the expected reduction in missed class time or midweek games.
- How the summer fits (between the spring championship and the fall preseason) is not addressed beyond the transition-period rules.

## 3. Championship (College Cup): timing, site, selection, bracket

### Takeaway
The College Cup would move to the **spring**. **Specific dates, site and bracket size are not yet determined.** The NCAA says the oversight committee is working with NCAA staff and broadcast partners to identify a spring date. The **2026** DI and DIII men's championships remain in December under the current model.

### Cited Findings
- "If adopted, this proposal would move the Division I Men's Soccer College Cup to the spring segment." — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q5)

- During the rescission period the oversight committee is working with NCAA staff and broadcast partners to identify a possible spring championship date. No date is named. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- **Championship selection** (including eligibility of midseason enrollees) was one of the 20 opposing conferences' concerns. — [NCAA-Jun24](https://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1)
- **Current model, 2026:** the 2026-27 DI recruiting calendar sets a men's soccer dead period "DEC 11–14, Friday through Monday of the NCAA Division I Men's Soccer Championship." That confirms the 2026 College Cup is in December under the current model. — [NCAA-RecCal-2627](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026%E2%80%9327D1Rec_OtherRecruitingCalendar.pdf) (T1)

## 4. Transfer portal / notification-of-transfer windows

### Takeaway
Today, DI soccer's transfer windows are tied to the fall season: a 30-day fall window plus the shared May 1-15 spring window for fall sports. Under the affirmed proposal, **DI men's soccer only** would move to **one 15-consecutive-day window opening the day after the spring championship**, starting 2027-28. **Women's soccer windows are not changing.** The **2026-27 windows (Nov. 23-Dec. 22, 2026 and May 1-15, 2027) are unaffected** for both genders.

### Cited Findings
- **Current (2026-27) windows**, both men's and women's soccer:
  - Fall window: **Nov. 23, 2026 – Dec. 22, 2026**.
  - Spring window for fall sports other than football: **May 1, 2027 – May 15, 2027**.
  - Source: [NCAA-TW-Fig13-1](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/d1/D1Comp_UndergraduateTransferWindows.pdf) (T1; file last modified 2026-09-09)
- **Historical 2025-26 windows:**
  - Men's soccer: Nov. 24 – Dec. 23, 2025.
  - Women's soccer: Nov. 17 – Dec. 16, 2025.
  - Spring window for fall sports: May 1-15, 2026.
  - Source: [NCAA-TW-2526](http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Transfer/DIUG_Windows.pdf) (T1, historical)
- **Current structure:** the men's soccer window is 45 days split into two windows. — [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1)
- **New structure:** starting 2027-28, there is one 15-consecutive-day spring window opening the day after the DI Men's Soccer Championship. — [NCAA-May13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1)
- If implemented, the men's soccer notification-of-transfer window would fall after the spring championship ends. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- The transfer-window change was remanded with the season proposal in June and was "expected to be adjusted to align with the new playing season." — [NCAA-Jun24](https://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1)
- **Midyear transfers:** if adopted, the competition-in-year-of-transfer rule would bar a men's soccer player who competed for a DI school in the fall from competing for a new DI program in the following winter/spring term(s). — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q9)
- **How the window works:**
  - A player must notify within the sport's window.
  - Compliance enters the name in the portal within two business days.
  - Contact from other schools can begin only after the name is in the portal.
  - After notification, the current school may cancel or reduce athletics aid for the next term, and is not obliged to take the player back.
  - Source: [NCAA-NOT](http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/Notification_of_Transfer.pdf) (T1)

### Inferences
- **Derivation:** for current DI men's players, the 2026-27 fall and spring windows are the last ones under the two-window structure, if the proposal survives rescission. The first single post-College-Cup window would fall in spring 2028 (end of the 2027-28 season). The exact dates depend on the not-yet-set championship date.

### Gaps
- The exact 2027-28 men's soccer window dates are not set (the championship date is TBD).
- No document found says whether the fall-sports May 1-15 spring window would still apply to men's soccer, or whether any exceptions (e.g., head-coach departure) change. The NCAA statements describe "one" window.
- The assignment asked whether windows are tied to championship selection dates. NCAA-Jun24 and NCAA-May13 tie the new window to the **championship's conclusion**. A source stating the current fall-sport window formula (e.g., days after selection) was **not opened this session**. Tier 1 gap.

## 5. Recruiting calendar (contact/dead periods, visits, June 15 date)

### Takeaway
No NCAA document found says the decision changes DI soccer recruiting rules, contact dates or visit rules. The 2026-27 DI recruiting calendar (updated Aug. 4, 2026) still shows the current-model men's soccer dead periods, including one tied to the **December** College Cup. **The NCAA has not said whether or when the men's soccer recruiting calendar will be revised.**

### Cited Findings
- **2026-27 DI men's soccer recruiting calendar** (calendar year Aug. 1, 2026 – July 31, 2027):
  - Dead period Nov. 9-12 (Monday-Thursday of the initial week for the fall signing date for athletics aid agreements).
  - Dead period Dec. 11-14 (Friday-Monday of the DI Men's Soccer Championship; a coaching staff member may attend an event held in conjunction with, and in the host city of, the championship).
  - Quiet period Dec. 23-25.
  - Source: [NCAA-RecCal-2627](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026%E2%80%9327D1Rec_OtherRecruitingCalendar.pdf) (T1, "Updated: August 4, 2026")
- **2026-27 DI women's soccer recruiting calendar:**
  - Dead period Aug. 1-11.
  - Dead period Nov. 9-12 (fall signing week).
  - Dead period Dec. 16, 2026 – Jan. 6, 2027.
  - Dead period July 28-31.
  - **Caveat:** extracted from a multi-column PDF; the column alignment was checked but should be re-verified visually before publishing.
  - Source: [NCAA-RecCal-2627](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026%E2%80%9327D1Rec_OtherRecruitingCalendar.pdf) (T1)
- **Period definitions:**
  - Dead period: no in-person recruiting contacts or evaluations on or off campus, and no official or unofficial visits to campus.
  - Quiet period: in-person contact only on the member school's campus.

## 6. Roster limits, roster counting dates and financial aid (House settlement)

### Takeaway
DI roster limits for opt-in/defendant-conference schools are **28 for men's soccer and 28 for women's soccer** (Bylaw 17.2). The roster must be submitted by the day before the first contest that counts for championship selection, or Dec. 1, whichever is earlier. **No NCAA document found addresses how the two-semester season interacts with roster limits, counting dates, or financial aid.** The Q&A does document midyear-enrollee and midyear-transfer eligibility.

### Cited Findings
- **Bylaw 17.2 Roster Limitations:** Men's Soccer **28**, Women's Soccer **28**. These apply to each sport at a DI school that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement. Adopted 6/6/25, effective 7/1/25. — [LSDBi-17.2](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20250830&ruleId=106414) (T1)
- **Proposal 2025-17 (roster limitation provisions):** adopted June 23, 2025 as emergency legislation, effective July 1, 2025.
  - Bylaw 17.2.1: rosters must be submitted no later than the calendar day before the first contest used for championship selections, or Dec. 1, whichever is earlier.
  - Bylaw 17.2.4: the sport must stay within the limit through the end of the academic year or the end of the playing season, whichever is later.
  - Source: [LSDBi-2025-17](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108684) (T1)
- **House Q&A (June 13, 2025):**
  - Fall sports must be at or below roster limits by the end of the day before the first date of competition that counts for championship selection.
  - Winter/spring sports must comply by Dec. 1 or the day before the first selection-counting contest, whichever is earlier.
  - The playing season includes the postseason.
  - Designated Student-Athletes may exceed the limit.
  - Source: [NCAA-HouseQA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2024-25/June2025D1Gov_PhaseThreeInstSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q C1). That document also noted it was subject to change pending the June 23 Board vote; LSDBi-2025-17 shows final adoption.
- **Midyear replacement (House Q&A, current law):**
  - In **fall sports**, a rostered player who withdraws from the school between terms may be replaced on the roster during the current year.
  - In **winter and spring sports**, a player who withdraws may not be replaced after roster submission.
  - Source: [NCAA-HouseQA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2024-25/June2025D1Gov_PhaseThreeInstSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q C36)
- **Midyear enrollees / spring eligibility under the proposal:** a spring initial enrollee is eligible for the spring segment (Q10). A player who competed in the fall for one DI school cannot compete in the spring for another DI school (Q9). — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1)

### Inferences
- **Documented tension, no resolution found:** men's soccer is treated as a **fall sport** in current roster-limit and midyear-replacement rules, which tie deadlines to the first selection-counting contest. Under the new model the championship moves to spring and the season spans two terms. No NCAA source found says whether men's soccer will be treated as a fall or spring sport for roster submission, roster expiration ("end of the playing season, whichever is later") or midyear replacement. The opposing conferences' June concern about "eligibility for midseason enrollees" relates to this area.

### Gaps
- No T1 source on roster counting dates, midyear replacement, or settlement benefits/aid for men's soccer under the two-semester model.
- No T1 source on **spring-semester or summer athletics aid** effects (e.g., aid for a player who leaves in the post-College-Cup window). NCAA-NOT states the general rule that aid can be reduced or cancelled for the next term after notification.
- No source found on whether a spring College Cup extends the **"end of the playing season"** for roster-limit compliance into late spring (Bylaw 17.2.4 language). Not determined per any document found.

## 7. Which recruiting class is first under the new model (derivation)

### Takeaway
**Derivation, not an NCAA statement:** if it survives rescission, the model is effective **Aug. 1, 2027 (2027-28 season)**. The first freshmen to play an entire four-season college career under it would be those who **enroll in fall 2027**, typically the HS **class of 2027**. HS juniors (class of 2028) and younger would also be fully under it. Current DI men's players would see it from 2027-28 onward.

### Cited Findings
- Effective Aug. 1, 2027, before the 2027-28 playing season. Nothing is effective immediately. — [NCAA-QA](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1, Q2)
- Takes effect for the 2027-28 academic year if not rescinded. — [NCAA-Oct7](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)

### Inferences (all derived from the effective date above; label as derivation in any content)
- **HS class of 2027** (current seniors in fall 2026, enrolling fall 2027): first class whose **entire** college career would be under the two-semester model. All four seasons would be 2027-28 onward.
- **HS class of 2026** (2026-27 college freshmen): one season under the current model (fall 2026 with a December College Cup), then three under the new model.
- **Current DI sophomores in 2026-27:** one more current-model season, then two new-model seasons. **Current juniors:** one more current-model season, then one new-model season (2027-28). **Current seniors:** would finish under the current model unless they have remaining eligibility.
- **Women's soccer recruits of every class:** no change from this decision.
- The 2026 fall season and the December 2026 College Cup are unaffected for everyone.

### Gaps
- No NCAA guidance found for players with extra eligibility years, or for transfer timing in the transition year (2026-27 fall/spring windows into a 2027-28 single window).

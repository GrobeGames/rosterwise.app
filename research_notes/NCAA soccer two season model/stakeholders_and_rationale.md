# Stakeholder Positions and Stated Rationale: NCAA DI Men's Soccer Two-Semester (Fall/Spring) Model

Research date: 2026-10-08 (all sources below were opened and read on 2026-10-08 unless marked otherwise).
Tier key (RosterWise content standards): T1 = governing body or institution publishing the fact itself; T2 = established news reporting an event or a named on-record quote (events and quotes only); T3 = reference, used only to find primaries. Banned sources (recruiting services, ranking/aggregator sites, forums, unofficial social posts) were excluded. Where a banned or aggregator page came up in search, it is named under Gaps so the writer knows it was seen and deliberately not used.

**Anchor finding (correcting the unverified premise).** "An NCAA committee approved soccer to become a fall and spring sport" is only partly accurate:
1. The change is **Division I men's soccer only**. Women's soccer is not included.
2. The DI Men's Soccer Oversight Committee **adopted** it on 2026-05-13.
3. The DI Cabinet **remanded** it on 2026-06-24.
4. The Cabinet **affirmed** it on 2026-10-07 with less than 75% of the vote, so it is now in a **30-day rescission period**.
5. As of 2026-10-08 it is **not yet final**.

## Q1. What did the NCAA actually approve, and what did named officials and governance bodies say?

### Takeaway
NCAA.org (T1) documents three steps: committee adoption on May 13, Cabinet remand on June 24, and Cabinet affirmation on Oct 7, which was short of 75% and so triggered rescission. **None of the three NCAA releases contains a direct quote from a named NCAA official or committee chair.** The NCAA's stated rationale appears only as unattributed release text or as attributions to "advocates" and "student-athlete representatives."

### Cited Findings

**A. Committee adoption, 2026-05-13 (T1)**
- **Action and timing.** The DI Men's Soccer Oversight Committee "adopted legislation changing the sport's playing and practice season to two semesters, effective Aug. 1, 2027." The action was subject to DI Cabinet review at its June 23-24 meeting. The committee had introduced the model in January under the new DI governance structure. — [NCAA.org, "Committee adopts 2-semester playing season for DI men's soccer," NCAA Staff, 2026-05-13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1)
- **Season structure.**
  - Fall segment: late August to the Saturday before Thanksgiving, up to 18 contests.
  - Spring segment: starts mid-February, up to 10 contests before the championship.
  - The 25-contest total is unchanged. The championship moves to spring, with postseason dates "to be determined later."
  - Today's season, for comparison, is a 10- to 13-week fall window ending with a December College Cup.
  - Source: same NCAA.org release, 2026-05-13 (T1).
- **Transfer window.** Starting in 2027-28, men's soccer would have a single 15-consecutive-day notification-of-transfer window in the spring, opening the day after the DI championship. This replaces the current 45 days split across two windows. — same NCAA.org release, 2026-05-13 (T1)
- **The release has no named-speaker quotes, no mention of women's soccer, and no study or data cited.** This is the content of the release as read; see the stated rationale in Q3.

**B. Cabinet remand, 2026-06-24 (T1)**
- **Remand.** The DI Cabinet remanded the playing-season proposal, and the accompanying transfer-window change, to the oversight committee. The Cabinet "asked the committee to revisit the proposal to provide additional clarity." — [NCAA.org, "DI Cabinet remands two-semester men's soccer proposal to oversight committee for further review," Meghan Durham Wright, 2026-06-24](http://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1). The quoted phrase is confirmed via the Times Union's quotation of the release (see the T2 entry below).
- **Opposition.** The proposal "was opposed by 20 Division I conferences" over the duration of the season, the lack of clarity about related rules, and the impact on championship selections, including eligibility of midseason enrollees. The Cabinet also raised concerns about the student-athlete academic experience. — same NCAA.org release, 2026-06-24 (T1)
- **Student-athlete view.** The same release states that men's soccer student-athletes broadly support the change. — same NCAA.org release (T1)
- **No vote count and no named speakers** appear in the release.

**C. Cabinet affirmation, 2026-10-07 (T1)**
- **Headline: "DI Cabinet narrowly affirms men's soccer calendar proposal."** The Cabinet reviewed and affirmed the proposal on Wednesday, Oct. 7, but not with the 75% needed to bypass rescission. — [NCAA.org, Meghan Durham Wright, 2026-10-07](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- **Why it moved forward after the remand.** In June the Cabinet had asked for more information on midyear enrollees and on school-level scheduling. The committee provided that feedback, and the Cabinet was "generally satisfied that the new schedule would be manageable." — same release (T1)
- **Next step.** During the rescission period the oversight committee will work with NCAA staff and broadcast partners to identify when the spring championship might occur. — same release (T1)
- **No vote tally and no named-speaker quotes.** — same release (T1)

**D. Formal legislation, Proposal No. 2026-27 (T1)**
- **Title and intent.** The proposal is titled "Playing and Practice Seasons – Soccer – Length of Playing Season." It applies "in men's soccer" and keeps the 132-day playing season and the 25-contest maximum. It lets the championship segment span two semesters or three quarters and shifts the national championship to the spring term. All aspects are effective August 1, 2027. — [NCAA, "Question and Answer Resource, NCAA Division I Proposal No. 2026-27" (PDF, undated)](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1)
- **Contest limits per segment** (Q&A No. 6):
  - Fall: maximum 18 contests (15 regular-season plus 3 scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum 8 regular-season contests.
  - Spring: maximum 10 contests (8 regular-season plus 2 scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum 3 regular-season contests.
  - Scrimmages do not count toward the minimums (Q&A No. 7).
  - Source: same PDF (T1).
- **Conference championships.** Their timing does not change. Conferences may choose to hold them in the fall or spring segment (Q&A No. 8). — same PDF (T1)
- **Midyear transfers.** Under the competition-in-year-of-transfer rule, a player who transfers at midyear after competing in the fall could not compete for the new school in the spring (Q&A No. 9). — same PDF (T1)
- **Midyear initial enrollees.** A first-time student who enrolls full-time at midyear can compete in the spring if otherwise eligible (Q&A No. 10). — same PDF (T1)
- **Rest days and transition periods.**
  - The proposal adds 2 consecutive days off in the fall segment and 3 in the spring. These do not count toward the 132 days (Q&A Nos. 21-22).
  - It also creates an optional 7-day transition period before each segment, which likewise does not count toward the 132 days (Q&A Nos. 11-15).
  - Source: same PDF (T1).
- **Relationship to the U.S. Soccer white paper.** The NCAA states that the proposal is "a distinct and independent proposal" introduced by the Men's Soccer Oversight Committee, not the U.S. Soccer NextGen white paper. Unlike that paper, it does not expand the playing season and has no regionalized tiering or promotion/relegation (Q&A No. 3). — same PDF (T1)

It quoted the NCAA release ("The Cabinet asked the committee to revisit the proposal to provide additional clarity"). It reported that Metro Atlantic Athletic Conference commissioner Travis Tellitocci confirmed his conference was among the opponents; this is paraphrased, not a direct quote. — [Times Union via Yahoo Sports, Mark Singelais, "NCAA doesn't approve move to 2-semester men's soccer season," dated 2026-06-25 on page](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/ncaa-doesnt-approve-move-2-173000861.html) (T2). The page returned HTTP 403, so the body and any quotes were not read.**

### Inferences
- The user's premise needs two corrections before publication. First, the change is men's-only. Second, "approved" is not yet final: it is a committee adoption plus a Cabinet affirmation, now inside a rescission window.
- The NCAA's own releases attribute rationale institutionally, not to named officials. The site can quote the NCAA (the release text) but cannot attribute the reasoning to a named NCAA official or committee chair from these documents.

### Gaps
- **No named NCAA official, Men's Soccer Oversight Committee chair, or Cabinet chair quoted** in any T1 document found. The committee's chair was not identified.
- **No Cabinet vote count** was published. "Narrowly" and "less than 75%" are the only descriptors.
- **The 20 opposing conferences are not named by the NCAA.** Only the MAAC was named, and only in T2 reporting.
- **The Q&A PDF is undated.** I could not confirm whether it reflects the version after the June remand, as amended for the October Cabinet vote, or the earlier May version. A post-remand amendment, if any, was not located.

## Q2. Did men's and women's soccer take different positions? Is the change for both?

### Takeaway
**The change is men's only.** Per NCAA.org (T1), the DI Women's Soccer Oversight Committee reviewed the proposal and declined to propose a similar change. Women's soccer instead adopted a different reform in 2024-25: an earlier start and a two-week-longer season, with the College Cup moved to the second full weekend of December starting in 2026. I found **no 2026 primary or on-record source** that states a collective position of women's soccer coaches on a two-semester model. The often-repeated claim that women's coaches are "less supportive" remains **unverified**.

### Cited Findings
- "The Division I Women's Soccer Oversight Committee reviewed the proposal and declined to propose a similar change" (paraphrased in the fetch output; the substance is confirmed). — [NCAA.org, 2026-10-07](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- Q&A No. 1 asks whether the proposal applies to both men's and women's soccer. The answer is "No," and it does not affect women's legislation. Women's soccer is governed by "recently adopted NCAA Division I Proposal No. 2024-62 (Playing and Practice Seasons -- Women's Soccer -- First Date of Competition and Transition Period)" and a January 17, 2025 educational column. — [NCAA Proposal 2026-27 Q&A PDF](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2026-27D1Gov_Proposal2026-27QuestionandAnswer.pdf) (T1)
- **Historical, 2025-04-14.** The DI Sports Oversight Committee moved the Women's College Cup to the "second full weekend in December" beginning in 2026, the same weekend as the Men's College Cup. This followed legislation lengthening the women's playing season by two weeks, intended to give more rest and reduce missed class time. No named quotes. — [NCAA.org, Greg Johnson, "DI Women's Soccer Championship schedule to shift, beginning in 2026," 2025-04-14](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-di-womens-soccer-championship-schedule-to-shift-beginning-in-2026/) (T1, historical)
- **U.S. Soccer's NextGen committee recommended different paths by gender (historical, Oct 2025).**
  - It said men's and women's soccer "should follow distinct implementation pathways and timelines."
  - For men: "moves expeditiously to a regionalized, two-tier competitive structure and a full academic year calendar."
  - For women:
    1. Keep the 2026 calendar that starts two weeks earlier, in July.
    2. Add "a new, separate spring 2027 competition for certain elite programs," which the release calls a U-23 spring pilot.
    3. Hold a broader engagement period to study more fundamental change, including a full-academic-year option.
  - It recommended "a more phased implementation timeline than we have recommended for the men."
  - Source: [U.S. Soccer / NextGen College Soccer Committee, "Ensuring College Soccer Thrives in the Modern Era…," white paper, October 2025, pp. 8, 13-14](https://www.soccerwire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/NextGen-College-Soccer-White-Paper_vFINAL.pdf) (T1 as a U.S. Soccer document, historical). **Host caveat:** the copy read is hosted on soccerwire.com. See Gaps.
- **The white paper's stated reasons for the gender difference (U.S. Soccer, T1, historical).**
  - Program counts: 350 women's DI programs vs 213 men's.
  - National-team roster share with college experience: about 70% of the 2025 SheBelieves Cup USWNT roster vs about 38% of the 2025 Gold Cup USMNT roster.
  - Spread of champions since 2000: men 15 different champions, 5 from non-power conferences; women 9 champions, all but 2 from legacy power conferences.
  - Conference sponsorship: women's soccer is sponsored by 31 of 32 multisport conferences.
  - Source: same white paper, pp. 4-5 (historical).
- **The white paper's view on applying the women's calendar to men.** It reports "universal feedback" that applying the 2026 women's calendar (July start) to the men's game would not meet the committee's objectives for men. — same white paper, p. 13 (historical)
- **Women's soccer coverage in October 2026 reporting.** T2 reporting on the October Cabinet vote that mentions women's soccer simply repeats the NCAA fact that the women's committee declined. Pittsburgh Soccer Now, a regional soccer outlet (see the reliability note in Q6), said Pitt's Ben Waldrum and Randy Waldrum support the two-semester model. The article paraphrases rather than quotes them and does not clearly state which program each represents. — [Pittsburgh Soccer Now, John Krysinsky, 2026-10-08](https://pittsburghsoccernow.com/2026/10/08/ncaa-moves-one-step-closer-to-year-round-mens-soccer-model-what-does-this-mean-for-pitt-duquesne-robert-morris-usl-and-the-american-soccer-ecosystem) (T2, low weight)

### Inferences
- The women's Oversight Committee's decision not to propose a parallel change is the only T1-documented "women's position." That is a governance decision, not a statement of coaches' sentiment.
- Women's soccer took a different reform route (an earlier start and longer fall season), which predates the men's proposal.

### Gaps
- **No on-record 2026 statement from women's DI coaches as a group** was found, whether from the United Soccer Coaches women's DI committee or from named women's head coaches, for or against a two-semester model. The historical claim that women's coaches are less supportive is **unverified** in permitted sources. One result (topdrawersoccer.com, "Women's coaches support two-semester model," undated, with a quoted Anson Dorrance) points the *opposite* way. TopDrawerSoccer is a recruiting/ranking site, so it is **banned and was not used**; it is mentioned only so the writer does not repeat the "less supportive" claim as fact.
- I did not locate the text of Proposal 2024-62 or the 2025-01-17 educational column (out of scope; covered by the legislative-history researcher).

## Q3. What evidence and rationale did proponents cite?

### Takeaway
The NCAA's stated rationale (T1) is qualitative: student-athlete well-being, less missed class, more recovery time, and more predictable schedules. **No NCAA document read cites an injury study or dataset.** The only quantitative case comes from the **U.S. Soccer NextGen white paper (Oct 2025)**, which cites:
- cost-saving estimates;
- U-19 pro-contract counts from U.S. Soccer's internal registration database;
- international-player shares.

The NCAA states explicitly that its proposal is independent of that paper. Pro-pathway alignment (MLS Next Pro, the calendar) appears in U.S. Soccer and coach commentary, not in NCAA rationale.

### Cited Findings

**NCAA stated rationale (T1)**
- The May 13 release lists these benefits:
  - a more balanced academic and athletic experience, by reducing time demands in any single term;
  - a less compressed season with potentially fewer midweek games, meaning less missed class time and support for academic performance and degree completion;
  - more predictable weekly schedules;
  - more recovery time between matches, which "could support injury prevention and return-to-play protocol";
  - stronger campus integration.
  - Source: [NCAA.org, 2026-05-13](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-committee-adopts-2-semester-playing-season-for-di-mens-soccer/) (T1). The "injury prevention and return-to-play protocol" wording is also quoted by the [Philadelphia Inquirer, 2026-07-17](https://www.inquirer.com/college-sports/division-one-mens-soccer-ncaa-two-semester-season-philly-coaches-20260717.html) (T2).
- The Oct 7 release says stakeholders in the men's soccer community "have long supported the change as a better way to develop talent." Advocates, "including student-athlete representatives on the Cabinet," argue it allows more rest and recovery between games and reduces weekday travel. — [NCAA.org, 2026-10-07](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)
- **No study, injury dataset, or missed-class figure is cited** in either NCAA release. — NCAA.org releases of 2026-05-13 and 2026-10-07 (T1, as read)

**U.S. Soccer NextGen College Soccer Committee white paper (T1 as U.S. Soccer's own document; historical, Oct 2025)**
- **Release and named quotes.** U.S. Soccer released the paper on 2025-10-16, with these quotes:
  - JT Batson, U.S. Soccer CEO: "This isn't about rewriting college soccer, it's about renewing it."
  - Cindy Parlow Cone, U.S. Soccer President: "we need to proactively determine changes that will have a positive impact."
  - Dan Helfrich, NextGen Committee chair: "It's a modernization plan that strengthens well-being, reduces costs, and creates new opportunities for growth."
  - Source: [U.S. Soccer, "U.S. Soccer and NextGen College Soccer Committee Release White Paper to Strengthen the College Game," 2025-10-16](https://ussoccer.com/stories/2025/10/us-soccer-nextgen-college-soccer-committee-release-white-paper-strengthen-college-game) (T1, historical)
- **Committee membership.** The committee had 17 members. Named members include:
  - Dan Helfrich (Deloitte);
  - Ali Curtis (President of MLS NEXT Pro);
  - Adrian Hanauer (Seattle Sounders/Reign);
  - Sarah Jones Simmer (NWSL COO);
  - Jeremy Alumbaugh (USL Championship president);
  - Patrick Kraft (Penn State AD);
  - John Wildhack (Syracuse AD);
  - Darryll Pines (University of Maryland president);
  - Chris Clunie (Davidson AD).
  - Advisors were Ray Reid (retired men's coach), Robbie Church (retired women's coach), and a men's and a women's college player.
  - Source: [White paper, pp. 2-3](https://www.soccerwire.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/NextGen-College-Soccer-White-Paper_vFINAL.pdf) (historical)
- **Problems the paper names.** It cites "the physical, mental, and academic toll of a highly congested single semester fall soccer calendar" and "the absence of highly competitive and meaningful matches for elite players between December and May." Its context list includes "Increasing financial pressure on college athletic programs … in the wake of the settlement of the House litigation" and "more opportunities for teenagers to bypass college soccer." — same, p. 4
- **Recommended men's model.** Most matches would be played Friday to Sunday, with no more than 3 regular-season matches in any 14-day period. — same, p. 10
- **Cost claim.** "Preliminary estimates of operational cost savings range from $25K for programs that compete primarily locally today, to $150-$350K for programs with a national travel footprint." This is attributed to regionalization and to eliminating summer preseason. The NCAA's adopted proposal does *not* include regionalization. — same, p. 12
- **Pro-pathway data, men.** U.S. Soccer's internal registration database counts U-19 men signing pro contracts in U.S. leagues: 50 in 2020 and 123 in 2025, a "146% overall increase." The paper notes "MLS Next Pro was introduced in 2022, likely contributing to the large YoY growth." — same, p. 16
- **Pro-pathway data, women.** U-19 women signing pro contracts: 2 in 2022, 16 in 2024, 10 in 2025. — same, pp. 16-17
- **International-player share.** International players make up 34.3% of men's DI rosters vs 11.7% of women's (2024). — same, p. 17
- **Women's pro-pathway shares.** About 50% of DI domestic pro league players on the men's side come from college, vs about 85% on the women's side. The paper attributes this to "last available NCAA RPI Ranking numbers." — same, p. 15
- **Injury data.** **The white paper cites no injury study or injury dataset.** Its well-being claims are qualitative. — same, as read

**Coaches and players: proponent arguments (T2 quotes)**

He calls his preferred framework the "21st Century Model." — [Pittsburgh Soccer Now, 2026-10-08](https://pittsburghsoccernow.com/2026/10/08/ncaa-moves-one-step-closer-to-year-round-mens-soccer-model-what-does-this-mean-for-pitt-duquesne-robert-morris-usl-and-the-american-soccer-ecosystem) (T2, low weight, paraphrase only)

**U.S. Soccer reaction to the Oct 7 vote (T2-reported social post)**

He credited the U.S. Soccer NextGen College Committee, wrote "College soccer is a uniquely American institution," and called it "a huge congrats." — [Front Row Soccer, Michael Lewis, 2026-10-07](https://www.frontrowsoccer.com/2026/10/07/two-semester-season-ncaa-approves-new-d-i-mens-setup-beginning-in-august/) (T2). **The X post itself was not opened**; unofficial social posts are excluded, but this one is from an official's account. ### Inferences

## Q4. What concerns or opposition were raised on the record?

### Takeaway
The only T1-documented opposition is institutional:
- 20 DI conferences, unnamed, opposed in June, over season length, rule clarity, and championship selection/midyear-enrollee eligibility;
- the Cabinet's own academic-experience concern.

Named on-record concerns come from T2-quoted coaches:
- travel cost;
- February weather and preseason timing;
- overuse injury;
- shared facilities and staff;
- small-school voice in decisions.

**No on-record concerns were found about Title IX, international students, multisport athletes, or House-settlement budgets.** Budget pressure appears only as context in the U.S. Soccer white paper.

### Cited Findings
- **Conference opposition (June).** The proposal was opposed by 20 DI conferences over "the duration of the season," "the lack of clarity about related rules," and the impact on championship selections, including eligibility for midseason enrollees. — [NCAA.org, 2026-06-24](http://www.ncaa.org/di-cabinet-remands-two-semester-mens-soccer-proposal-to-oversight-committee-for-further-review/) (T1). The quoted phrases are confirmed in the [Philadelphia Inquirer, 2026-07-17](https://www.inquirer.com/college-sports/division-one-mens-soccer-ncaa-two-semester-season-philly-coaches-20260717.html) (T2).
- **The Cabinet's own concern.** The Cabinet raised concerns about what a two-semester season "would mean for the student-athlete academic experience." — NCAA.org 2026-06-24 (T1), as quoted by the Times Union and the Inquirer (T2)
- **Named conference.** The Metro Atlantic Athletic Conference was among the opposing conferences; commissioner Travis Tellitocci confirmed this, paraphrased by the Times Union. No direct quote. — [Times Union via Yahoo Sports, 2026-06-25](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/ncaa-doesnt-approve-move-2-173000861.html) (T2)

## Q5. Vote count, rescission/override mechanism, and next governance dates

### Takeaway
No vote count was published. The rule is in a **30-day rescission period beginning 2026-10-07**. It is defeated only if **two-thirds of DI members (at least 245 schools and/or conferences)** vote to rescind. Otherwise it takes effect for 2027-28, with legislation effective Aug. 1, 2027. The NCAA has not published the end date of the window.

### Cited Findings
- The Cabinet affirmed the proposal "but not with the 75% approval needed to bypass the rescission process." The proposal enters a 30-day rescission period in which eligible DI schools or conferences can vote to defeat it. If two-thirds of DI members (at least 245 schools and/or conferences) do not rescind, it takes effect starting in the 2027-28 academic year. — [NCAA.org, 2026-10-07](https://www.ncaa.org/news/di-cabinet-narrowly-affirms-mens-soccer-calendar-proposal/) (T1)

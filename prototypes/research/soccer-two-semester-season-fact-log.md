## B. What the model would change (men's soccer, from 2027-28 if not rescinded)

| Claim | Primary source | Verified | Articles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nothing is effective immediately**; all aspects become effective **Aug. 1, 2027**, prior to the 2027-28 playing season. | NCAA Q&A, Q2 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester, mens-timeline, mens-index |
| The **132-day playing season** limit is maintained. | NCAA Q&A, Q4 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| The **25-contest maximum** (including regular-season contests and scrimmages/exhibitions) is maintained. | NCAA Q&A, Q6; NCAA.org 2026-10-07 ("maintaining the current limit on total games per year at 25") | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Fall segment:** maximum **18** contests (**15** regular-season + **3** scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum **8** regular-season contests. | NCAA Q&A, Q6 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| Fall segment runs "starting in late August and concluding **the week before Thanksgiving**." | NCAA.org, 2026-10-07 (the May 13 release says "until the Saturday before Thanksgiving" — copy uses the October/affirmed wording) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Spring segment:** maximum **10** contests (**8** regular-season + **2** scrimmages/exhibitions); minimum **3** regular-season contests; begins in **mid-February**. | NCAA Q&A, Q6; NCAA.org 2026-10-07 and 2026-05-13 (mid-February) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| Scrimmages/exhibitions **do not count** toward the minimums. | NCAA Q&A, Q7 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| Because the segment maximums (18 + 10 = 28) exceed the 25-contest annual cap, a team cannot play the maximum in both segments. | Arithmetic from NCAA Q&A, Q6 figures (sourced consequence, §5.2) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| The **Division I Men's Soccer College Cup moves to the spring segment**. | NCAA Q&A, Q5; NCAA.org 2026-10-07 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester, mens-timeline, mens-index |
| **Championship dates are not set.** During the rescission period the oversight committee will work "with NCAA staff and broadcast partners to identify when the championship might occur during the spring semester." | NCAA.org, 2026-10-07; NCAA.org 2026-05-13 ("specific dates for the postseason will be determined later") | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Current format, for comparison:** a "10- to 13-week fall window," with the Men's College Cup in **December**. | NCAA.org, 2026-05-13 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| The proposal **does not change when conference championships occur**; it allows each conference to decide whether its championship is in the fall or spring segment. | NCAA Q&A, Q8 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Transfer window:** starting in 2027-28, **one 15-consecutive-day window in the spring**, opening the day after the DI Men's Soccer Championship, replacing the current **45 days split into two windows**. | NCAA.org, 2026-05-13. The Oct. 7 release confirms only that the window would occur "after the conclusion of the championship in the spring" — the 15-day figure rests on the May release (see Open items) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Midyear transfers:** the competition-in-year-of-transfer rule would bar a player who competed for one DI school in the fall from competing for a new DI program in the winter/spring term(s) following transfer. | NCAA Q&A, Q9 (Bylaw 14.5.5.2) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Midyear initial enrollees** are eligible for the spring segment if they register full-time for that term, attend the first day of classes, and are otherwise academically and athletically eligible. | NCAA Q&A, Q10 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| An **optional 7-day transition period** may precede each segment (fall: from the Thursday of the 14th weekend before Thanksgiving; spring: from the Friday of the 12th weekend before the College Cup); it does not count toward the 132 days. | NCAA Q&A, Q11–Q15 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Two additional consecutive days off** in the fall segment and **three** in the spring segment; they do not count toward the 132 days and countable athletically related activity is prohibited on them. | NCAA Q&A, Q21–Q22 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| Unlike U.S. Soccer's **October 2025 NextGen College Soccer Committee White Paper**, the proposal does not include an expansion of the playing and practice season or a national regionalized schedule with tiering or promotion/relegation; NCAA calls it "a distinct and independent proposal." | NCAA Q&A, Q3 (white paper itself not cited — its readable copy sat on a host that may be banned) | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Stated benefits (NCAA, May release):** a more balanced academic and athletic experience with lower time demands in any single term; potentially fewer midweek games and less missed class; more predictable weekly schedules; more recovery time between matches; greater integration into campus life. | NCAA.org, 2026-05-13 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |
| **Supporters' case (NCAA, Oct. release):** long supported in the men's soccer community "as a better way to develop talent"; advocates, "including student-athlete representatives who serve on the Cabinet," argue it allows more rest and recovery between games and reduced weekday travel. | NCAA.org, 2026-10-07 | 2026-10-08 | blog-two-semester |

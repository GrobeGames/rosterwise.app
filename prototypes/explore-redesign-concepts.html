RosterWise · Volleyball

Explore Tab — Five Redesign Concepts

Each concept keeps the existing information architecture (Search Programs, Divisions, Conferences, Connections, Program Intel, and the headline stats) but reaches for a more distinctive, premium feel. The volleyball identity is expressed through geometry — court lines, the net, serve trajectories, rotation zones, and a box-score data language — never through emoji. Navy + gold is treated as the premium brand pairing, with the existing blue as a secondary accent.

1

Center Court

Bento grid

Technique: an asymmetric bento layout with tiles of varied size and weight, unified by a faint full-bleed court line-drawing that bleeds behind every tile — so the whole screen reads as one court.

Why it works: bento is the current premium-app standard; the shared court substrate makes it unmistakably volleyball and ties otherwise-separate cards into a single composition. Navy/gold reads more upscale than the current flat gradient cards.

2

The Net

Editorial

Technique: a calm, magazine-style layout on cream — a large Playfair serif headline, generous whitespace, and a recurring net-tape hairline that divides each section the way the net divides a court.

Why it works: it feels editorial and trustworthy rather than “app-y,” differentiating RosterWise from competitor dashboards. The net motif is subtle and structural, and the light palette photographs beautifully for the App Store.

3

Court Zones

Spatial nav

Technique: the six navigation destinations are laid out as the six rotational zones of a volleyball half-court. Tapping a zone navigates — the screen literally is a court.

Why it works: nothing else on the App Store navigates like this; it’s instantly volleyball and memorable. Best as a signature “hero” treatment — pair with a scrollable list below for power users. (Highest concept risk, highest distinctiveness.)

4

Stat Line

Data dashboard

Technique: a data-forward, dark “analyst terminal” — monospaced tabular numerals, animated counters, sparkline trends, and a box-score visual language with each destination shown as a stat row.

Why it works: RosterWise’s wedge is depth of roster data — this leans all the way into that. It signals “serious tool” to recruiting families and reads very differently from soft marketing UIs. Sparklines hint at the trend analysis to come.

5

Trajectory

Glassmorphism

Technique: frosted translucent cards float over a soft navy color-mesh, with a single gold serve-arc sweeping behind the hero. Depth comes from blur, layering, and inner highlights.

Why it works: it’s the most modern, “iOS-native” of the five and feels premium and energetic without emoji. The serve trajectory is the one literal volleyball cue, used as motion rather than decoration. Translates cleanly to soccer (swap arc for a pass line).

My recommendation: Concept 1 (Center Court / Bento) is the safest high-impact upgrade — it keeps today’s structure and tap targets but feels dramatically more premium, and the shared court substrate is a reusable brand device. Concept 5 (Trajectory / Glass) is the most contemporary and ports cleanly to the soccer app. Concept 3 (Court Zones) is the boldest brand statement if you want something no competitor has — best piloted as an A/B test. Concepts 2 and 4 are strong divergent directions (editorial vs. analyst) if you want to reposition tone. All five avoid emoji and express volleyball through geometry, not literal balls.
